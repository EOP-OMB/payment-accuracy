- Answer: "The OIG concluded that HHS was non-compliant with several PIIA requirements\
    \ in FY 2025, identifying nine findings across ten risk-susceptible programs.\
    \ The following summarizes the OIG's conclusions and recommendations for each\
    \ non-compliant criterion and the actions and measurable milestones HHS has planned\
    \ to achieve compliance.\n\nRisk Assessment Coverage — Agency-Wide (Finding #1):\
    \ The OIG found that HHS did not conduct improper payment risk assessments for\
    \ all programs with annual outlays greater than $10 million at least once every\
    \ three years. The OIG recommended that HHS continue to enhance and implement\
    \ policies and procedures to fulfill the three-year assessment requirement and\
    \ work with OMB to implement an approach that meets the intent of PIIA. In response,\
    \ HHS updated its program inventory in FY 2026 to align with the Federal Program\
    \ Inventory. HHS is committed to achieving full assessment coverage by FY 2028\
    \ through process improvements including extending the risk assessment period,\
    \ maintaining a complete cycle-status inventory, streamlining the risk assessment\
    \ questionnaire, and applying a modified approach for programs with outlays between\
    \ $10 million and $100 million.\n\nTANF — No Estimate Reported (Finding #2): The\
    \ OIG found that HHS did not calculate or report an improper payment estimate\
    \ for TANF due to statutory limitations under 42 U.S.C. § 617, which preclude\
    \ HHS from requiring states to participate in a TANF improper payment measurement.\
    \ The OIG recommended that HHS continue advocating for legislative changes and\
    \ work with OMB and other stakeholders to develop and implement an approach to\
    \ calculate and report a TANF estimate. HHS continues to advocate for the necessary\
    \ legislative authority and works with OMB and Congress to identify viable pathways\
    \ to full PIIA compliance for the TANF program. In the absence of measurement\
    \ authority, HHS supports state program integrity through fraud risk assessments,\
    \ PARIS data matching, and enforcement of the Systematic Alien Verification for\
    \ Entitlements system.\n\nFoster Care — Incomplete State Coverage (Finding #3):\
    \ The OIG found that the Foster Care improper payment estimate does not currently\
    \ encompass the full population of states, as HHS uses a three-year review cycle\
    \ and has completed reviews in only 26 states since resuming Title IV-E reviews\
    \ in FY 2024. The OIG recommended that HHS continue to follow its plan to conduct\
    \ Title IV-E reviews so that all states are included in the estimate by FY 2027.\
    \ HHS now anticipates completing reviews covering all 50 states, the District\
    \ of Columbia, and Puerto Rico by FY 2028, at which point a complete baseline\
    \ and reduction target will be established and reported. This timeline reflects\
    \ a delay resulting from the implementation of a new information system that temporarily\
    \ reduced the volume of Title IV-E claims available to support the required review\
    \ sample. In FY 2026, HHS expanded review coverage, conducted 16 Title IV-E reviewer\
    \ training sessions, and provided quality improvement assistance to states through\
    \ Program Improvement Plans.\n\nHead Start — Rate Above 10 Percent (Finding #4):\
    \ The OIG found that the Head Start improper payment rate of 10.298 percent in\
    \ FY 2025 exceeded the 10 percent PIIA compliance threshold and recommended that\
    \ HHS focus on root causes and evaluate critical and feasible corrective actions\
    \ to bring the rate below 10 percent. Following the initial measurement, HHS conducted\
    \ a formal final adjudication review of RY 2025 transactions, allowing recipients\
    \ additional time to submit required documentation and resolve improper payments\
    \ identified during the initial review. Upon receipt of additional documentation,\
    \ all 173 unknown payments were resolved, and the final adjudicated RY 2025 rate\
    \ was 0.84 percent, demonstrating that the initial rate reflected documentation\
    \ access challenges rather than underlying payment inaccuracy. In FY 2026, HHS\
    \ implemented process changes to address these challenges, including lengthening\
    \ the review timeline, deploying a data validation tool that reduced recipient\
    \ follow-ups, and delivering specialized documentation training attended by review\
    \ participants. HHS's measurable milestone is to bring the Head Start improper\
    \ payment rate below 10 percent and sustain that level in future reporting cycles.\n\
    \nMedicare Part C — Recovery Audit Delays (Finding #5): The OIG found that RADV\
    \ audit recoveries had not yet been made or reported for payment years 2018 and\
    \ 2019 and recommended that HHS improve recovery audit efforts and explore alternative\
    \ recovery vehicles if RADV cannot effectively serve as the sole recovery audit\
    \ strategy. In FY 2026, HHS initiated 1,553 new RADV audits covering payment years\
    \ 2020, 2021, and 2024 through an expanded audit methodology, and issued audit\
    \ findings for payment years 2014–2015. On September 25, 2025, the U.S. District\
    \ Court for the Northern District of Texas vacated CMS's 2023 RADV Final Rule,\
    \ which would have allowed HHS to extrapolate audit results across an entire contract\
    \ population and recover substantial amounts. HHS appealed this decision on November\
    \ 21, 2025 and will fully comply with the district court's order as long as it\
    \ remains in effect while continuing to pursue upcoming RADV payment year audits.\n\
    \nAPTC — State-based Exchange Measurement Gap (Finding #6): The OIG found that\
    \ HHS has not calculated or reported an improper payment estimate for State-based\
    \ Exchanges and recommended that HHS continue working with OMB and stakeholders\
    \ to implement the measurement program. \nIn May 2026, HHS published the Notice\
    \ of Benefit and Payment Parameters for 2027 final rule, which included provisions\
    \ to implement the State Exchange Improper Payment Measurement program. HHS plans\
    \ to begin sampling and estimation on January 1, 2027, and report its first improper\
    \ payment rate in FY 2028.\n\n\nMedicare Part C — Demonstrated Improvements (Finding\
    \ #7): The OIG found that HHS did not effectively demonstrate improvements to\
    \ the Medicare Part C payment integrity rate in FY 2025, noting that the rate\
    \ increased from 5.61 percent in FY 2024 to 6.09 percent in FY 2025. The OIG recommended\
    \ that HHS refine corrective action plans focused on medical record discrepancies\
    \ and evaluate critical and feasible action steps to improve reduction targets.\
    \ In FY 2026, HHS continued RADV audits as its primary corrective action, initiated\
    \ a targeted review of In-Home Health Risk Assessment diagnoses for 35 contracts,\
    \ and continued outreach to approximately 1,016 Medicare Part C and Part D contracts\
    \ through Health Plan Management System memos on audit findings and best practices.\
    \ For detail on FY 2026 plan sponsor audit and outreach activities, see Finding\
    \ #8 below. HHS notes that at a 95 percent confidence level the confidence intervals\
    \ for FY 2024 (4.93–6.29 percent) and FY 2025 (5.40–6.78 percent) overlap significantly,\
    \ meaning the observed difference does not constitute a statistically meaningful\
    \ change. \n\nMedicare Part D — Demonstrated Improvements (Finding #8): The OIG\
    \ found that HHS did not effectively demonstrate improvements to the Medicare\
    \ Part D payment integrity rate in FY 2025, noting that the rate increased from\
    \ 3.70 percent in FY 2024 to 4.00 percent in FY 2025. The OIG recommended that\
    \ HHS enhance corrective action plans focused on plan sponsor documentation and\
    \ evaluate critical and feasible action steps to improve reduction targets. In\
    \ FY 2026, HHS conducted three national plan sponsor audits with one completed\
    \ audit recovering $617,772, and continued outreach reaching approximately 1,016\
    \ Medicare Part C and Part D contracts through Health Plan Management System memos\
    \ on high-risk drugs, audit findings, and best practices. As with Medicare Part\
    \ C, HHS notes that the confidence intervals for FY 2024 (3.31–4.15 percent) and\
    \ FY 2025 (3.75–4.46 percent) overlap, and the observed difference does not constitute\
    \ a statistically meaningful change. \n\nMedicaid and CHIP — Demonstrated Improvements\
    \ (Finding #9): The OIG found that HHS did not effectively demonstrate improvements\
    \ to the Medicaid and CHIP payment integrity rates in FY 2025, with Medicaid increasing\
    \ from 5.09 percent to 6.12 percent and CHIP increasing from 6.11 percent to 7.05\
    \ percent. The OIG recommended that HHS enhance corrective action plans focused\
    \ on missing documentation, state noncompliance, and improper eligibility determinations,\
    \ and work with states to improve payment accuracy. HHS notes that these increases\
    \ are directly attributable to the unwinding of COVID-19 public health emergency\
    \ flexibilities, which required states to simultaneously resume full eligibility\
    \ redetermination processes for tens of millions of beneficiaries. In FY 2026,\
    \ HHS conducted onsite visits to 43 states to assess provider screening and enrollment\
    \ compliance, initiated audits in seven states to review whether states determined\
    \ beneficiary eligibility correctly, and completed 18 state Medicaid Eligibility\
    \ Quality Control reviews of Calendar Year 2025 eligibility determinations. HHS\
    \ does not expect FY 2026 corrective actions to be fully reflected in the measured\
    \ rate until FY 2028, when the first cycle of states measured after the conclusion\
    \ of COVID-19 flexibilities will be included in the estimate."

- Answer: The performance agreements for accountable officials at HHS include expectations
    related to improving program integrity, reducing improper payments, and demonstrating
    measurable progress toward resolving areas of non-compliance identified by the
    OIG in its annual PIIA compliance report. For programs found non-compliant in
    FY 2025 — including those with rates above the 10 percent threshold, incomplete
    measurement coverage, or insufficient demonstrated improvements — accountable
    officials are expected to implement and advance the corrective actions and measurable
    milestones described in HHS's annual compliance plans submitted through the PaymentAccuracy.gov
    data call process. Successful achievement of these goals supports positive performance
    evaluations and may contribute to eligibility for performance-based recognition,
    while failure to demonstrate meaningful progress toward bringing programs into
    compliance with PIIA requirements may result in negative performance reviews or
    reduced consideration for performance-based incentives. By linking performance
    expectations to the successful remediation of non-compliant criteria these agreements
    reinforce accountability and ensure that officials prioritize actions needed to
    achieve compliance across their respective programs.
