    Sufficiency: 'Public Assistance is designed to reimburse everything from an hour
      of overtime to the replacement of an entire wastewater treatment facility. It
      is designed to be flexible and support the range of recovery needs. The program
      delivery model, built on standard roles and processes, enables the Federal Emergency
      Management Agency (FEMA) to employ a risk-based approach that recognizes that
      not all applicants or projects require the same level of resources, documentation,
      or oversight. FEMA adjusts its approach based on risks. The risk-based approach
      balances developing policy, delivering the program, and prioritizing resources
      to maintain an efficient use of resources. FEMA prioritizes resources and effort
      where risk and complexity are high. That means ensuring personnel and technical
      resources are made available, and administrative oversight is more in-depth,
      where a disaster, applicant, or project represents a higher risk in one or more
      of the four risk areas–Mission, Finances, Program, and Reputation. By contrast,
      FEMA limits level of effort, resources, and administrative oversight where risk
      is low. That means assigning fewer personnel or technical resources and decreasing
      requirements and level of review where an applicant or project does not pose
      a significant risk in one or more of the four risk areas.


      FEMA’s corrective action plan addresses the root-cause type of documentation
      availability and payment-validation deficiencies, which primarily result in
      unknown payments when recipients or subrecipients do not provide sufficient
      records to validate drawdown amounts. This root cause has historically accounted
      for more than 90 percent of the total reported payment error value. FEMA’s approach
      is proportionate to the severity of the documentation-related error amount and
      rate because it directs resources to the transactions most likely to affect
      the program’s improper-payment and unknown-payment results without imposing
      unnecessary burden on lower-risk recipients. FEMA will continue to monitor error
      trends and documentation results through payment-integrity reviews and adjust
      corrective actions if the associated dollar amount or error rate does not decline.
      FEMA remains committed to streamlining and automating the Public Assistance
