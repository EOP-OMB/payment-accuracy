    Accountability: The improper payments reported for the agency are made at the
      grantee level. All recipients and subrecipients per Grant Terms and Conditions
      are required to complete Funds Management Training, Fraud Awareness Training,
      National Criminal History Check (NSCHC) training, and Internal Control Training
      annually. These trainings targets root causes in both unknown and overpayment
      categories to address adequate record keeping and controls to ensure payments
      are correct and eligibility is properly documented. Additionally, according
      to Grant Terms and Conditions, grant recipients have an obligation to make proper
      payments, i.e. payments to the right individuals, at the right time, in the
      right amounts, and to document these payments in accordance with laws, regulations,
      and agency policy. Grant recipients identified as having improper payments must
      assess the root cause drivers of these payments and remediate the conditions
      that led to the improper payment. Failure to properly address the root cause
      drivers of improper payments may result in additional monitoring activities
      and financial penalties. Prioritizing effective stewardship of federal resources
      is part of the agency’s strategic plan and the focus of executive managers,
      programs, and agency staff.

    Needs1: As part of its strategic plan, AmeriCorps is prioritizing effective stewardship
      of federal resources by improving its business processes and systems. To accomplish
      this objective, AmeriCorps will strengthen internal controls, reporting systems,
      and audit readiness. AmeriCorps will improve its business processes to support
      the integrity of financial information, promote accountability, and prevent
      fraud. AmeriCorps has the internal controls, human capital, and information
      systems necessary to implement targeted corrective action plans focused on reducing

    Needs2: No resources were requested. The agency did not request resources because
      the payments are generally not made at the agency level and are made by individual
      grantees. Corrective actions to target grantees, such as trainings and the NSCHC
      vendor mandate, can be developed and performed with resources available.
    Sufficiency: The improper payments reported in ASN and FGP are made at the grantee
      level. The improper payments in ASN and FGP are not made at the agency level
      and largely do not represent monetary loss to the government. The majority are
      unknown payments for which the agency’s grantees are unable to provide sufficient
      support documentation to determine if the payment was proper or improper. Since
      the agency does not have direct control over the payments, AmeriCorps has introduced
      baseline monitoring and corrective actions required for recipients who have
      improper payments. In FY27, AmeriCorps will introduce an additional level of
      “advanced” monitoring activities for organizations who are high risk and have
      identified improper payments. It is essential that grantees have access to training
      and resources that provide information on financial grants management. Having
      effective and readily available resources to guide grantees in program implementation
      promotes effective stewardship of federal funds and reduces non-compliance.
      The resources created build upon the agency’s currently available tools and
      focus specifically on areas of non-compliance.
