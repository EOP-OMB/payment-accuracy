    Answer: 'The primary cause of improper payments in the Army''s DTS program is
      a significant monetary loss, totaling over $116 million year-to-date, stemming
      from failures to validate entitlements. These are not simply procedural errors
      but actual financial losses to the government. The top drivers of these monetary
      losses are overpayments for travel at the permanent duty station (PDS), Meals
      and Incidental Expenses (M&IE), and lodging. These issues point to a systemic
      failure by Certifying Officers to perform basic validation of rates and calculations
      before approving vouchers for payment. This category of error represents a direct
      loss of taxpayer funds and is a primary reason the program is designated as
      an OMB High-Priority Program.


      A separate, but equally critical, issue is the high volume of "unknown" payments,
      which total over $120 million year-to-date. The root cause for this is a fundamental
      breakdown in pre-payment internal controls, specifically the failure of certifying
      officers to verify that required supporting documentation, such as receipts,
      is attached to travel vouchers before approval. According to the Corrective
      Action Plan, this procedural failure, where vouchers are marked "pay ready"
      without mandatory receipts, is the principal driver of unknown payments. According
      to PIIA guidelines, a payment that cannot be substantiated with documentation
      is classified as an improper payment, even if the payment was otherwise valid.


      These two primary issues are exacerbated by systemic and data-related problems.
      The DTS system itself lacks hard-coded controls or hard-stop edits to prevent
      the submission and approval of vouchers that are missing mandatory documentation
      or contain clear calculation errors; the system currently provides only soft
      warnings that can be bypassed. This technological gap enables persistent human
      non-compliance. Furthermore, a lack of seamless data integration between DTS
      and systems that house source documents like travel authorizations frequently
      results in incomplete records. This makes it impossible for post-payment auditors
      to verify the legality and accuracy of payments, thus categorizing them as "unknown."
