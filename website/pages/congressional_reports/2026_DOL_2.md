    Answer: "Since February 2025, the Department of Labor's (DOL) Employment and Training\
      \ Administration (ETA) has approached strengthening Unemployment Insurance (UI)\
      \ program integrity and reducing IPs through a multi-layered strategy emphasizing\
      \ transparency, accountability, and innovation. Key actions include holding\
      \ states accountable for performance, investing in systemwide improvements,\
      \ expanding access to training and data resources, strengthening ID verification,\
      \ partnering with DOL’s Office of Inspector General (OIG), and issuing program\
      \ guidance. These efforts are outlined in ETA's UI Integrity Strategic Plan,\
      \ which contains the UI antifraud and IP reduction strategies.\n\nIn FY 2026,\
      \ DOL took aggressive action to hold states accountable for combatting fraud,\
      \ addressing high IP rates, and improving UI program performance. In February,\
      \ ETA sent individual notices to each state regarding specific performance measures,\
      \ including the IP rate, and the resulting level of engagement they could expect\
      \ from ETA as a result of this performance. In March, ETA convened state and\
      \ federal leaders from all 53 state workforce agencies to discuss performance,\
      \ integrity initiatives, policy issues, and reemployment strategies. Between\
      \ March and September, ETA deployed UI Strike Teams to the worst-performing\
      \ states, resulting in 22 onsite engagements and one additional engagement to\
      \ assess to what extent fraud was impacting the state’s UI program. In June,\
      \ ETA launched public State Performance Scorecards (https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/performance/StatePerfScorecard.html),\
      \ tracking metrics like IP and fraud rates and benefit timeliness measures.\
      \  In July, ETA issued model national UI standards for states to adopt to improve\
      \ UI integrity, following a letter from the Secretary to state governors demanding\
      \ action on fraud and performance. Results from the UI Strike Team engagements\
      \ and ETA’s model national UI standards are being incorporated into state FY\
      \ 2027 State Quality Service Plans (SQSP) (see UIPL No. 14-26).\n\nETA also\
      \ leveraged funding to support integrity goals. The Reemployment Services and\
      \ Eligibility Assessment (RESEA) program is aimed at improving employment outcomes\
      \ and reducing fraud. In March 2026, ETA awarded over $38 million in grants\
      \ to 46 states that met or exceeded their RESEA duration and reemployment outcome\
      \ targets for FY 2025. In July 2026, ETA announced the latest Operating Guidance\
      \ (see UIPL No. 13-26) and is providing states with over $415.6 million in grants\
      \ to continue operation of their RESEA programs. \n \nETA deepened collaboration\
      \ with the DOL OIG to safeguard taxpayer dollars and address UI fraud and performance\
      \ issues. The partnership included regular meetings on emerging fraud trends,\
      \ coordination with states, financial institutions, and unclaimed property agencies\
      \ to recover unspent pandemic-era UI funds on prepaid debit cards, and joint\
      \ engagement through UI Strike Team visits to strengthen DOL OIG and state relationships.\
      \ DOL also published a final rule requiring states to share confidential UI\
      \ information with federal officials, including DOL-OIG ensuring proper oversight\
      \ and accountability for the UI program.\n\nFinally, ETA advanced technology\
      \ data programs and ID verification infrastructure. It deployed Unemployment.gov\
      \ as an informational website in January and is set to launch an initial pilot\
      \ in early FY 2027, providing a national front door for benefit applications\
      \ with built-in fraud checks. It also upgraded ID verification by moving Login.gov\
      \ to a higher assurance level and integrating USPS in-person proofing, having\
      \ verified over 532,000 IDs and proofed over 377,000 others since February 2025.\
      \ Additionally, the Integrity Data Hub (IDH) a multi-state fraud-detection data\
      \ system, expanded access to Treasury's Do Not Pay (DNP) database and enhanced\
      \ tools for identifying employer tax fraud schemes.\n"

- Answer: "ETA is committed to holding states accountable for recovering UI overpayments\
    \ and, where appropriate, providing states with tools and resources to enhance\
    \ such efforts, including through its partnership with OIG. Overpayment recovery\
    \ is critical to protect both state UI trust funds and federal funds. ETA has\
    \ reminded states that overpayment recovery must be given the same priority as\
    \ fraud prevention and detection activities. States identify and establish overpayments\
    \ for recovery using methods such as cross-matching and data analytics. Federal\
    \ law requires that states use certain recovery methods to collect benefit overpayments,\
    \ including: \n•\tBenefit Offsets (Title II, Subtitle A of the Middle-Class Tax\
    \ Relief and Job Creation Act of 2012, UIPL No. 05-13, and Section 5 of UIPL No.\
    \ 13- 20, Change 2); \n•\tTreasury Offset Program (TOP) for applicable covered\
    \ debt (Bipartisan Budget Act of 2013, UIPL Nos. 02-19, 12-14, and 04-20);\n•\t\
    Cross Program Offset Recovery Agreement (CPORA) (UIPL No. 05-13); and\n•\tInterstate\
    \ Reciprocal Offset Recovery Arrangement (IRORA) (UIPL No. 05-13). \nETA also\
    \ strongly recommends states use additional overpayment recovery activities, where\
    \ allowed by state law, to improve recovery outcomes. Additional overpayment recovery\
    \ actions, could include, but are not limited to, voluntary payment plans, wage\
    \ garnishments, and interception of state tax refunds. State UI laws may provide\
    \ for additional overpayment recovery actions, such as voluntary payment plans,\
    \ wage garnishments, interception of state tax refunds, etc.\n"
