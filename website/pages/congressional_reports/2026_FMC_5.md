- Answer: The Federal Maritime Commission (FMC) has determined that conducting payment
    recovery audits is not cost-effective for its programs based on the agency's limited
    payment volume, historically low improper payment risk, and established internal
    control framework. The Commission's expenditures primarily consist of payroll,
    travel, contracts, and interagency agreements, which are subject to routine pre-payment
    reviews, financial reconciliations, and management oversight. Given the nature
    and volume of these transactions, the anticipated costs of conducting formal recovery
    audits, including administrative resources, personnel time, and audit-related
    expenses, are expected to exceed the potential recoverable amounts. The FMC continues
    to monitor payment activity, investigate identified discrepancies, and pursue
    recovery of overpayments when appropriate through existing financial management
    processes. Accordingly, the Commission considers its current risk-based monitoring
    and recovery procedures a more efficient and economical approach to safeguarding
    federal funds while maintaining compliance with applicable payment integrity requirements.
