    Answer: 'USDA reimburses participating Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP)
      providers for the cost of serving program meals to eligible children. USDA does
      not reimburse providers directly. Instead, USDA issues payment to the states.
      The states then redistribute those funds to CACFP program sponsors and independent
      centers. Sponsors reimburse centers and day care homes under their supervision.


      A significant source of program error is committed by household applicants for
      program benefits and the sponsors who process those applications. Sponsoring
      organizations also make errors in determining whether day care homes and certain
      child care centers are located in area eligible sites; area eligibility determines
      the level of reimbursement that this subset of providers are entitled to for
      each program meal.


      Additional error occurs in the process of counting and aggregating meals for
      federal reimbursement. Meal aggregation is a responsibility of all program sponsors
      and independent centers.'

    Answer: 'USDA reimburses participating schools and institutions for the cost of
      serving program meals to eligible children. USDA does not issue benefits to
      schools or school districts (school food authorities, or SFAs) directly. Instead,
      USDA issues payment to the states. The states then redistribute those funds
      to SFAs.


      The primary source of improper payments in the National School Lunch Program
      (NSLP) and School Breakfast Program (SBP) are errors committed by applicants
      and school districts in the process of certifying children''s eligibility for
      program benefits. Certifying children for eligibility requires collecting and
      reviewing income information on household applications or confirming children’s
      eligibility through participation in other means-tested programs. In both cases,
      the root cause of error is the failure to access, manage, or submit data or
      other eligibility information.


      Household applicants make mistakes in reporting household size and income. In
      addition to household error, school district officials make mistakes in reviewing
      applications, summing income amounts, and comparing that information to the
      program’s eligibility standards. Household reporting and application processing
      are, by far, the most error prone elements of certification. Nevertheless, there
      is some error associated with the process of certifying children for school
      meal benefits through data matching. Both state and school district officials
      sometimes fail to match an applicant to the dataset of households and/or children
      who receive benefits through one of several other programs that confer categorical
      eligibility for free school meals.


      A secondary source of improper payments errors is committed by schools and school
      districts in counting and claiming meals for federal reimbursement. Claiming
      meals for USDA reimbursement requires summing and transmitting reimbursable
      meal counts by schools to SFAs, by SFAs to state agencies, and by states to
      USDA. Error in this process is uncommon. Nevertheless, to the extent that schools,
      SFAs, or states rely on manual input, summation, or review of meal counts, there
      remains a risk for error. These errors, like certification errors, are caused
      by a failure to properly access or manage data on reimbursable meal counts.'

    Answer: 'USDA reimburses participating schools and institutions for the cost of
      serving program meals to eligible children. USDA does not issue benefits to
      schools or school districts (school food authorities, or SFAs) directly. Instead,
      USDA issues payment to the states. The states then redistribute those funds
      to SFAs.


      The primary source of improper payments in the National School Lunch Program
      (NSLP) and School Breakfast Program (SBP) are errors committed by applicants
      and school districts in the process of certifying children''s eligibility for
      program benefits. Certifying children for eligibility requires collecting and
      reviewing income information on household applications or confirming children’s
      eligibility through participation in other means-tested programs. In both cases,
      the root cause of error is the failure to access, manage, or submit data or
      other eligibility information.


      Household applicants make mistakes in reporting household size and income. In
      addition to household error, school district officials make mistakes in reviewing
      applications, summing income amounts, and comparing that information to the
      program’s eligibility standards. Household reporting and application processing
      are, by far, the most error prone elements of certification. Nevertheless, there
      is some error associated with the process of certifying children for school
      meal benefits through data matching. Both state and school district officials
      sometimes fail to match an applicant to the dataset of households and/or children
      who receive benefits through one of several other programs that confer categorical
      eligibility for free school meals.


      A secondary source of improper payments errors is committed by schools and school
      districts in counting and claiming meals for federal reimbursement. Claiming
      meals for USDA reimbursement requires summing and transmitting reimbursable
      meal counts by schools to SFAs, by SFAs to state agencies, and by states to
      USDA. Error in this process is uncommon. Nevertheless, to the extent that schools,
      SFAs, or states rely on manual input, summation, or review of meal counts, there
      remains a risk for error. These errors, like certification errors, are caused
      by a failure to properly access or manage data on reimbursable meal counts.'
