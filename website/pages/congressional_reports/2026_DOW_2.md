    Answer: 'To address the root causes of improper payments, the Army is implementing
      a dual-track corrective action strategy that pivots from training to an enforceable
      accountability framework. To combat the more than $120 million in "unknown"
      payments caused by a breakdown in document verification, the Army will use its
      Risk Management and Internal Control (RMIC) program to enforce compliance. A
      key action, formalized by a FRAGO to be issued by December 15, 2026, will mandate
      that select commands perform quarterly transaction-level testing of voucher
      review controls within their eGRC system. The results of this testing will be
      compiled into a final report by June 30, 2027. Any failures identified during
      this testing will trigger formal, system-logged deficiencies that commanders
      must address, directly linking compliance to command accountability and their
      annual Statement of Assurance.


      To address the over $116 million in monetary losses, the Army is launching a
      parallel effort focused on a deep-dive analysis of the highest-dollar error
      categories. A joint Army-DFAS working group will conduct a Monetary Loss Root
      Cause Analysis, with a report due by November 30, 2026. This analysis will identify
      the specific drivers of overpayments for meals, lodging, and other expenses.
      Following this, by January 15, 2027, the team will map the entire end-to-end
      process for establishing and collecting debts from these overpayments. The findings
      will be used to develop a list of targeted corrective actions to streamline
      and enforce collections, which will be documented by March 31, 2027.


      To ensure sustained oversight for both unknown payments and monetary losses,
      the Army will utilize the monthly DFAS Post Pay PIIA DTS Travel Customer Update
      meeting, starting in October 2026. This forum will provide command leadership
      with direct, monthly feedback on their specific PIIA sampling error lists from
      DFAS, creating a continuous loop of correction and accountability. The overall
      Corrective Action Plan is set for full implementation by September 30, 2027,
      with the Army projecting it will achieve full PIIA compliance by successfully
      executing these targeted actions.

    Answer: 'The Department of the Air Force (DAF) has implemented a multi-faceted
      corrective action plan to address the root causes of improper and unknown payments
      within the Defense Travel System (DTS). These actions target the primary drivers
      of errors - process/procedural issues and human error - which contributed to
      a combined $248.8 million in improper and unknown payments in FY26. A key initiative,
      scheduled for completion by January 30, 2027, involves creating a new SAV (Staff
      Assistance Visit) checklist. This tool will be used to verify that Lead Defense
      Travel Administrators (LDTAs) are actively spot-checking vouchers and utilizing
      the Compliance Tool, directly addressing the procedural gap of insufficient
      oversight that leads to payment errors.


      To combat the human error element, which manifests as a failure to follow instructions
      and a lack of understanding of policies, the DAF has mandated continued, targeted
      training. This includes mandatory training for all newly assigned Approving
      Officials (AOs) and remedial training for any AO who approves three or more
      improper payments. These ongoing training sessions, with milestones tracked
      quarterly starting January 30, 2027, focus specifically on high-error areas
      such as validating receipts, correctly applying per diem rates, and managing
      verbal orders. To enforce these standards, a BOT will be run quarterly to remove
      system permissions for non-compliant AOs, ensuring that only properly trained
      and appointed individuals can approve travel payments.


      To address the significant issue of unknown payments, which are driven by a
      failure to provide valid supporting documentation, several process improvements
      will be implemented by March 31, 2027. These include publishing a standardized
      "Variations Authorized" memo, removing confusing location codes from DTS, and
      posting a "Certification of Lost Receipt" form on a DAF-wide forum to make it
      more accessible. Furthermore, to accelerate the resolution of unknown payments,
      a new process will be established to immediately notify travelers and AOs as
      soon as these errors are identified in post-payment reviews, rather than waiting
      for a formal report. These combined actions aim to reduce errors by simplifying
      processes, enhancing user knowledge, and enforcing compliance, with the goal
      of reaching full PIIA compliance by FY2027.

      '

    plan for risk analysis and the performance of payment recovery audits. PRAs are
    reviews and analyses of program accounting and financial records, supporting documentation,
    and other pertinent information supporting its payments; and it is designed specifically,
    to identify overpayments. It is through this system of internal controls surrounding
    the payments that assists in resolving the erroneous non-intentional monetary
