- Answer: As an independent agent, SSS does not have an Office of Inspector General
    (OIG) to evaluate improper payments. However, an external auditor conducted the
    PIIA audit for FY 2024.  The audit concluded that SSS was compliant with the applicable
    PIIA requirements and submitted required information to paymentaccuracy.gov; however,
    SSS did not complete an improper payment risk assessment in a timely manner for
    the program with annual outlays greater than $10 million, as required at least
    once every three years. SSS finalized the risk assessment during the 2024 audit.
    Since the risk assessment completed during the audit, no further corrective actions
    were necessary. For the next risk assessment cycle, SSS will ensure that the assessment
    is completed on time to maintain compliance.

- Answer: The performance agreements for the responsible official do not contain explicit
    incentives or consequences tied directly to achieving Payment Integrity Information
    Act (PIIA) compliance. Instead, compliance responsibilities are embedded within
    established managerial performance expectations requiring officials to fulfill
    all statutory and regulatory requirements, including the timely remediation of
    deficiencies identified in payment‑integrity processes. In the absence of formal
    incentives or consequences, the official is expected to take timely and effective
    action and maintain oversight of corrective measures as part of routine management
    duties. Accordingly, the agency’s progress toward compliance is assessed through
    managerial accountability, internal oversight, collaboration with program offices,
    and a sustained commitment to meeting federal requirements rather than through
    performance‑based incentives or consequences.
