    Answer: 'Audit actions taken included special audits and focused reviews of targeted
      problem areas. These reviews assessed errors related to SSA income matching,
      dependency, other income sources, and medical expenses by improving the accuracy
      of income verification and ensuring claims processors adhered to policies and
      procedures when determining pension rates. From June 6–7, 2026, the program
      conducted a virtual site visit with the St. Paul, Minnesota, Pension Management
      Center that included a review of 60 claims and identified errors causing improper
      payments. Findings were shared with the Pension Management Center during the
      visit and later included in the final report issued on July 30, 2026. Audit
      activities also occurred in FY 2024 and FY 2025 contributing to reductions of
      $104.05 million in SSA income match errors, $16.61 million in dependency errors,
      and $10.33 million in medical expense errors. The program will continue conducting
      special audits and focused reviews in FY 2027.


      Automation actions taken included conducting automated data matches with SSA
      to identify variances between the income SSA reported and the income beneficiaries
      reported to VA. Any differences indicated a potential change in a beneficiary’s
      income that could require an adjustment to their pension award. Validating these
      variances and making corrections when necessary help prevent future improper
      payments caused by SSA income match errors. Claims with identified variances
      were prioritized and sent to processors in batches so discrepancies could be
      resolved efficiently, benefits adjusted when warranted, and future overpayments
      minimized. Automated income matches were conducted on January 6, 2026, January
      22, 2026, May 7, 2026, and July 14, 2026. These corrective actions also occurred
      in FY 2024 and FY 2025 contributing to a reduction of $104.05 million in improper
      payments between FY 2025 and FY 2026. Quarterly SSA income matches will continue
      in FY 2027.


      Training actions taken included conducting targeted training to address SSA
      income errors and administrative issues related to dependency, other income
      sources, and medical expenses. Training was delivered during the virtual site
      visit with the St. Paul Pension Management Center on June 17, 2026. Training
      included a review of preliminary Payment Integrity Information Act of 2019 testing
      results for FY 2026, error rate trends, and error categories. Quality Calls
      with Pension Management Centers were held on March 17 and September 15, 2026
      covering SSA income match, root causes of user errors, and overall preliminary
      findings for FY 2026. Mitigation strategies for key root causes were also discussed.
      Training efforts also occurred in FY 2025 contributing to reductions of $104.05
      million for SSA income match errors, $16.61 million for dependency errors, and
      $10.33 million for medical expense errors. Training initiatives will continue
      in FY 2027 and will remain focused on preventing improper and unknown payments.


      Outcomes of these corrective actions taken in prior years have been effective
      and the program continues to see reductions in improper and unknown payment
      testing results. In FY 2027, the program will evaluate the effectiveness and
      impact of these corrective actions and determine whether they successfully mitigated
      the identified root causes.


      VA plans to continue these actions in FY 2027 and the expected completion date
      is September 30, 2027.'

    \ contract, which will audit claims for three programs: Civilian Health and Medical\
    \ Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs, Purchased Long Term Services\
    \ and Supports, and VA Community Care. Payments were tested to ensure the reimbursement\
    \ was based on the appropriate methodology and that the medical records support\
    \ the diagnostic related group billed for the services. \n\nDuring FY 2026, the\
    \ recovery audit identified overpayments where the medical record did not substantiate\
    \ the services billed or the coding was not accurate based on a review of documents.\
    \ Solutions to resolve these conditions are being developed but will not be implemented\
    \ until FY 2027. The identified overpayments resulted in re-adjudication of the\
    \ original payments in the claims processing system, leading to bills of collections\
    \ being issued to the community care providers. Additional methods used to recover\
    \ overpayments include internal off-sets and Treasury’s Invoice Payment Processing\
    \ Payment off-set.\n\nOffice of Revolving Funds \nThe Office of Revolving Funds\
    \ collaborates with the VA Office of Inspector General to perform recovery audits\
    \ on VA's Supply Fund program. Overpayments identified are mostly due to non-compliance\
    \ with contract billing requirements on federal supply schedule contracts. These\
    \ conditions are being resolved by education provided by the VA Office of Inspector\
    \ General to non-conforming vendors. The VA Office of Inspector General identifies\
    \ an overpayment, and a bill of collection is then prepared by the Office of Revolving\
    \ Funds with appropriate terms of collection. If the vendor chooses to dispute\
    \ the bill of collection VA will work towards appropriate resolution."
