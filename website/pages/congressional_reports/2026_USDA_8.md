- Answer: "For FY 2025, the USDA Office of Inspector General (OIG) determined that\
    \ USDA did not comply with three of the six Payment Integrity Information Act\
    \ of 2019 (PIIA) requirements. The OIG identified five noncompliant Phase 2 programs:\
    \ the Summer Food Service Program (SFSP), Emergency Conservation Program-Disasters\
    \ (ECP-Disasters), Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP), Supplemental Nutrition\
    \ Assistance Program (SNAP), and Noninsured Crop Disaster Assistance Program (NAP).\
    \ SFSP was noncompliant with Criterion 3, which requires publication of improper-payment\
    \ and unknown-payment estimates for Phase 2 programs. ECP-Disasters and CACFP\
    \ were noncompliant with Criterion 5, concerning payment-reduction targets and\
    \ demonstrated improvement, and Criterion 6, which requires a reported improper\
    \ payment rate below 10 percent. SNAP and NAP were noncompliant with Criterion\
    \ 6 because their reported improper payment rates exceeded the 10-percent threshold.\
    \ \n\n\nFSA Emergency Conservation Program-Disasters (ECP) \nECP was noncompliant\
    \ with Criterion 5 because it did not demonstrate improvement in payment integrity\
    \ or achieve its improper payment reduction target; the program was found noncompliant\
    \ with Criterion 6 because its FY 2025 IP/UP rate of 55.50 percent exceeded the\
    \ 10-percent statutory threshold. ECP was noncompliant for the fifth consecutive\
    \ fiscal year, subjecting the program to enhanced PIIA reporting requirements\
    \ on compliance activities taken or ongoing, new corrective actions, and a timeline\
    \ for achieving compliance. The OIG concluded that FSA had not effectively addressed\
    \ root causes nor implemented controls and corrective actions sufficient to achieve\
    \ the required payment integrity improvement, nor reduce the IP error rate below\
    \ 10 percent. The highest-frequency findings involved missing or inadequate producer-contribution\
    \ statements, missing performance certifications before payment, inadequate environmental\
    \ or conservation compliance documentation, inadequate cost-share itemization,\
    \ and insufficient evidence that financial-responsibility requirements were met.\
    \ For Criterion 5, the OIG recommended that FSA continuously improve mitigation\
    \ strategies, evaluate existing controls, implement stronger preventive and detective\
    \ controls, and take additional root-cause-based corrective actions when rates\
    \ do not improve. For Criterion 6, the OIG recommended that FSA and OCFO assess\
    \ the effectiveness of Agency- and State-level actions, monitor their implementation,\
    \ develop State-specific Corrective Action Plans, and target causes associated\
    \ with the highest improper payment percentages. FSA has implemented modernized\
    \ ECP software with automated payment calculations, State-specific payment scenarios,\
    \ geographic information system mapping, required document uploads, statutory\
    \ payment-limit controls, and automated performance-certification validation.\
    \ Milestones associated with Criteria 5 and 6 include the new software released\
    \ in January 2024, enhanced dashboard capabilities in October 2024, strengthened\
    \ administrative oversight using the FSA Form CCC-770 in July 2025, additional\
    \ software validations in June 2026, and planned completion of the Internal Review\
    \ and Documentation Tracking System (IRDTS) producer compliance review process\
    \ during 2026. FY 2026 testing of payments processed through the modernized system\
    \ is the principal effectiveness milestone for both criteria, but FSA should provide\
    \ a final projected compliance date, interim rate-reduction targets, and measures\
    \ demonstrating whether the actions reduced the program's rate below 10 percent.\n\
    \nNoninsured Crop Disaster Assistance Program  (NAP)\nNAP was found noncompliant\
    \ with Criterion 6 because its FY 2025 IP/UP rate was 10.56 percent. NAP exceeded\
    \ the 10 percent statutory threshold for the third consecutive fiscal year. The\
    \ OIG concluded that inadequate internal controls and corrective actions contributed\
    \ to recurring errors involving payment eligibility and limitations, verification\
    \ of payment accuracy, and missing or incomplete documentation. The highest-frequency\
    \ findings involved incorrect acres, colonies, or taps used in payment calculations;\
    \ missing producer signatures on required forms; incorrect harvested or appraised\
    \ production; and payments made when the payee did not share in the risk of producing\
    \ the crop. The OIG recommended that FSA evaluate whether corrective actions are\
    \ effective, monitor Agency- and State-level controls, develop targeted Corrective\
    \ Action Plans for recurring root causes, and complete the additional third-year\
    \ noncompliance reporting required by PIIA. To address Criterion 6, FSA revised\
    \ policies related to production reporting, commingled production, late-filed\
    \ acreage reporting; conducted trainings on grazing calculations and issued 15\
    \ national acreage-reporting training modules; and established recurring monthly\
    \ technical assistance calls with State NAP specialists through the end of FY\
    \ 2026. Completed Criterion 6 improvement milestones include policy changes in\
    \ April 2024, issuance of national acreage-reporting training in November 2024,\
    \ crop acreage-reporting system (CARS) software enhancements in March 2025, production\
    \ policy revisions in June 2025, and addition of FY 2025 testing data to the Internal\
    \ Controls PIIA Findings Dashboard in September 2025. Automation of the manual\
    \ Grass for Grazing calculation, intended to reduce manual data entry errors,\
    \ remains ongoing and is scheduled for implementation in late FY 2027. The CARS\
    \ software enhancements implemented for program year 2026 are expected to affect\
    \ NAP IP testing beginning in FY 2027. \n\nSummer Food Service Program (SFSP)\
    \ (Finding No. 25-01-FNA) \nSFSP was found to be non-compliant with criterion\
    \ 3 which requires the Food and Nutrition Administration (FNA) to publish improper\
    \ payment estimates for all programs and activities identified as susceptible\
    \ to improper payments under its risk assessment. The non-compliance was the result\
    \ of having not yet implemented a method to assess and report a rate. The Office\
    \ of the Inspector General (OIG) recommended that: 1) FNA continue work on the\
    \ planned study to develop an error rate for SFSP, which is described in FNA's\
    \ Sampling and Estimation Methodology Plan (S&EMP), and 2) FNA also report measurable\
    \ milestones to be accomplished in order to achieve compliance.\n\nBased on further\
    \ review of the options for SFSP, FNA determined that the SFSP data collected\
    \ to date (through the methodology outlined in prior year S&EMPs) was of sufficient\
    \ quality to support the development of an improper payment rate for fiscal year\
    \ (FY) 2025. The FNA has reported that estimated rate to the Office of Management\
    \ and Budget (OMB) for FY 2026 reporting purposes for publication on paymentaccuracy.gov.\
    \ For the longer term, FNA remains focused on reducing SFSP program error and\
    \ strengthening its improper payment reporting. To this end, FNA undertook new\
    \ research in FY 2026 that will, at a minimum, add to FNA's current understanding\
    \ of error in the program. The research begun in FY 2026 may also allow FNA to\
    \ eventually replace the current improper payment methodology with one that is\
    \ more efficient and cost effective. \n\nThe Child and Adult Care Food Program\
    \ (CACFP) (Finding No. 25-02-FNA)\nCACFP was found to be non-compliant with criterion\
    \ 5b which requires FNA to demonstrate improvements to payment integrity or reach\
    \ a tolerable improper payment (IP) and unknown payment (UP) rate. The non-compliance\
    \ was the result of CACFP not reaching its payment reduction IP/UP target rate\
    \ and not adequately demonstrating improvements to payment integrity. The OIG\
    \ recommended that the agency take the following actions to ensure compliance\
    \ with the PIIA: 1) Maintain a focus on continuous improvement in the development\
    \ and implementation of new mitigation strategies, and 2) where applicable and\
    \ cost effective, implement automated enhancements to better prevent and detect\
    \ errors. \n\nFNA concurred with the OIG recommendation but notes that the main\
    \ driver of the CACFP rate increase was the inclusion of an estimate for one type\
    \ of error in the CACFP Family Day Care Homes (FDCHs) that FNA had not previously\
    \ estimated or reported. The inclusion resolved a prior OIG non-compliance, but\
    \ as the rates between FY 2024 (FY 2025 reporting) and FY 2025 (FY 2026 reporting)\
    \ differ in scope they are not directly comparable. \n\nFNA continues to work\
    \ on strategies to reduce its CACFP error rate, including those underway prior\
    \ to issuance of the OIG recommendation. These include a comprehensive CACFP meal\
    \ claims validation effort begun in Spring 2026, where FNA staff visited CACFP\
    \ facilities and reviewed meal claim records and supporting documentation from\
    \ 10 states. This review identified instances of non-compliance with program rules\
    \ and/or inadequate recordkeeping in each of the project states. FNA is using\
    \ these findings to develop actionable reforms tailored to each state's specific\
    \ problems. FNA is also planning to apply broader lessons from the project to\
    \ the development of new or updated program guidance and possible long-term rulemaking\
    \ that focuses on root causes of error and possible automated validation checks.\
    \ \n\nThe Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and the Child and Adult\
    \ Care Food Program (CACFP)  (Finding No. 25-03-FNA)\nWere found to be non-compliant\
    \ with criterion 6 which requires FNA to achieve an IP and UP rate of less than\
    \ 10 percent. For SNAP, the non-compliance was the result of reporting a total\
    \ IP/UP rate of 10.93 percent, though this was a decrease from the previous year.\
    \ For CACFP, the non-compliance was the result of reporting a total IP/UP rate\
    \ of 10.50 percent, which was an increase of 1.67 percent from the previous year.\
    \ The OIG recommended that the agency take the following actions to ensure compliance\
    \ with PIIA: 1) Improve processes and efforts to evaluate the effectiveness of\
    \ corrective actions to assist in the reduction of the improper payments estimate\
    \ rate below the 10 percent threshold, 2) Re-evaluate and continue monitoring\
    \ agency-level corrective actions corresponding to the identified root causes\
    \ of improper payments, 3) Re-evaluate and continue ongoing monitoring of the\
    \ state-level corrective actions and ensure that these corrective actions are\
    \ being implemented, 4) Continue working with states to identify state-specific\
    \ corrective action plans and ensure that these corrective actions are being implemented,\
    \ and 5) Enhance corrective actions to specifically target and address the primary\
    \ root causes associated with the highest improper payment percentage.\n\nFor\
    \ SNAP, FNA continues to build on efforts to equip State agencies (SAs) with the\
    \ knowledge and tools needed to address the root causes of their SNAP payment\
    \ errors. FNA will conduct technical assistance and training to assist SAs in\
    \ identifying payment error causes and specific corrective actions to address\
    \ them. In addition, FNA will continue to share information about its Keys to\
    \ Payment Accuracy, which shares key strategies SAs have used to improve payment\
    \ accuracy. FNA also plans to continue to support SAs in improving earned income\
    \ verification, to address a common source of payment error nationwide.\n\nFor\
    \ CACFP, FNA concurred with the OIG recommendation but notes that a factor in\
    \ the CACFP rate increase to over 10 percent was the inclusion of an estimate\
    \ for one type of error in the CACFP Family Day Care Homes (FDCHs) that FNA had\
    \ not previously estimated or reported. The inclusion resolved a prior OIG non-compliance,\
    \ but as the rates between FY 2024 (FY 2025 reporting) and FY 2025 (FY 2026 reporting)\
    \ differ in scope they are not directly comparable. \n\nFNA continues to work\
    \ on strategies to reduce its CACFP error rate, including those underway prior\
    \ to issuance of the OIG recommendation. These include a comprehensive CACFP meal\
    \ claims validation effort begun in Spring 2026, where FNA staff visited CACFP\
    \ facilities and reviewed meal claim records and supporting documentation from\
    \ 10 states. This review identified instances of non-compliance with program rules\
    \ and/or inadequate recordkeeping in each of the project states. FNA is using\
    \ these findings to develop actionable reforms tailored to each state's specific\
    \ problems. FNA is also planning to apply broader lessons from the project to\
    \ the development of new or updated program guidance and possible long-term rulemaking\
    \ that focuses on root causes of error and possible automated validation checks.\
    \ \n\n\n"
