- Answer: DNFSB determined that conducting recovery audits is not cost effective because
    the FY 2026 Improper Payment Risk Assessment found that the agency’s only program
    with outlays above the $10 million threshold is not susceptible to improper or
    unknown payments. Since the program did not incur improper payments at levels
    that meet statutory thresholds for testing, the expected return from a recovery
    audit would not justify the cost of performing one. The Office of the Inspector
    General later confirmed these conclusions during its FY 2025 PIIA compliance audit,
    validating that a payment recapture audit would provide minimal benefit. As a
    result, DNFSB determined that resources are better allocated to maintaining existing
    internal controls rather than conducting recovery audits.
