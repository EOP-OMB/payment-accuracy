    Answer: 'RMA conducted a comprehensive analysis of current and historic Improper
      Payment Review (IPR) results to identify underlying error patterns, program
      vulnerabilities, and opportunities for targeted risk mitigation. Reviewed results
      associated with nonzero weighted improper payment dollars were analyzed using
      advanced data analytics and natural language processing techniques to evaluate
      narratives, identify recurring themes, and detect relationships among error
      conditions.


      Results were categorized and analyzed across multiple dimensions, including
      error type, crop insurance lifecycle stage, and associated improper payment
      dollars to identify potential root cause characteristics. The analysis identified
      a recurring sequence of errors, which subsequently increased the likelihood
      and magnitude of claim calculation errors. By identifying these relationships
      and recurring patterns, RMA obtained a more comprehensive understanding of the
      underlying factors contributing to improper payments. The results of this root
      cause analysis informed the development of targeted corrective actions, including
      enhancements to documentation and verification requirements, strengthened validation
      procedures, and expanded use of data analytics. This data-driven approach supports
      informed decision-making, strengthens program integrity.


      To address improper payments resulting from limited access to data and information,
      the Risk Management Agency (RMA) plans to implement a proactive program review
      process that identifies and notifies Approved Insurance Providers (AIPs) of
      potential share discrepancies after certification but before Federal Crop Insurance
      Corporation (FCIC) deadlines for policy corrections. This corrective action
      is intended to provide AIPs with sufficient time to investigate and resolve
      share discrepancies before they result in improper payments. The planned review
      process will serve as an early intervention control designed to identify and
      mitigate risks before payments are finalized. By identifying potential errors
      earlier in the process, RMA can reduce the likelihood of inaccurate payments,
      improve compliance with program requirements, and strengthen overall program
      administration. Development and implementation of this review process are currently
      planned, with full completion anticipated by December 2027.


      To further address the causes of improper payments and strengthen compliance
      with Standard Reinsurance Agreements (SRAs), RMA will implement a corrective
      action plan through the Approved Insurance Provider Review (APR) process. This
      effort will include a detailed evaluation of AIP quality control procedures
      to assess compliance with SRA requirements and the effectiveness of internal
      controls used to ensure payment accuracy. The review will focus on documentation,
      payment processing practices, and adherence to Appendix IV requirements. By
      conducting targeted analyses and increasing oversight of quality control processes,
      RMA will identify areas for improvement and require corrective actions where
      deficiencies are identified. Strengthened quality control reviews will help
      ensure that payment determinations are supported by complete and accurate documentation
      and that AIPs consistently follow established requirements.  Implementation
      of this corrective action is planned for completion by October 2027.

      '

    Answer: "Since the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) is administered\
      \ by State agencies, who also determine the structure of SNAP amongst numerous\
      \ policy options, waivers, and demonstration projects, corrective actions must\
      \ originate in and be tailored to each individual State agency's circumstances\
      \ and particular root causes of payment error. As such, the Food and Nutrition\
      \ Administration (FNA) is primarily focused on providing training and equipping\
      \ State agencies with the knowledge and tools needed to individually develop\
      \ meaningful root cause analysis and corrective action plans (CAPs), including\
      \ through the issuance of policy guidance.\n\nConsistent with SNAP regulations,\
      \ FNA requires States to enter into CAPs at the State level to address payment\
      \ error rates that exceed six percent, a standard established by law. States\
      \ are responsible for developing and implementing their own CAPs to address\
      \ program deficiencies that resulted in high improper payment rates. State CAPs\
      \ are based on each State’s own root cause analysis. State agencies follow FNA\
      \ guidance and conduct their own root cause analysis to identify the sources,\
      \ magnitude, and causes of deficiencies leading to improper payments. As States\
      \ conduct their root cause analysis, they are encouraged to request technical\
      \ assistance from FNA’s regional offices in interpreting data and identifying\
      \ root causes.\n\nIn addition, the Food and Nutrition Act of 2008, as amended,\
      \ provides disincentives in the form of financial penalties to encourage States\
      \ to accurately issue SNAP benefits and maintain low improper payment rates.\
      \ \n\nFinally, FNA is promulgating a proposed rule to implement provisions of\
      \ The Working Families Tax Cuts Act (Public Law 119-21) requiring certain States\
      \ to fund a portion of SNAP benefits they issue based on their payment error\
      \ rate. This change is designed to incentivize States to make more accurate\
      \ benefit payments. Matching requirements are based on a State’s payment error\
      \ rate and range from a State share of 0 to 15 percent of program allotments.\
      \ Requirements generally begin in Fiscal Year (FY) 2028."

- Answer: 'Overpayments are identified through RMA’s Compliance Activities and Results
    System (CARS) in accordance with Standard Reinsurance Agreement requirements.
    RMA utilizes the results of a variety of audits and reviews to compile data, conduct
    complex survey analytics, identify root causes, and determine the amount of improper
    payments. The data collected consists of but is not limited to; the type of error(s)
    that led to the improper payment, the stage at which the payment became improper,
    and who was responsible for the payment becoming improper. This comprehensive
    analysis allows RMA to identify the root cause of improper payments and develop
    an effective corrective action plan.


    For the Rural Housing Service (RHS), improper payments are identified through
    supervisory visits. Documentation is reviewed for accuracy and changes in income
    levels and individuals living in the residence.  If it is identified that a tenant
    was overpaid, they are advised and a receivable is established in the accounting
    system. Conditions that give rise to improper payments are if a tenant misstated
    their income or how many people are living in the unit or if the owner did not
    update the paper work to indicate that the unit was vacant. The supervisory visits,
    plus verifying wage match by state help catch and correct misstatements.


    The SFH-Claims Branch, Lender Quality Section performs loss claim post payment
    quality reviews (PQR) on lenders with the highest volume of loss claims processed
    during specified timeframes based on a statistical sampling.  The complete file
    including the origination package is reviewed for compliance. When lender errors
    are identified during the review, an initial findings letter is sent out, whereby
    the lender has an opportunity to provide additional documentation to dispute the
    findings. Once a final determination is made, a final findings letter is sent
    out and a receivable is set up to collect the funds. It must be noted that not
    all findings are errors.  The agency requests claim funds back if it is determined
    that adequate documentation was not provided.  Often, if the finding is minimal,
    the lender determines they don’t want to expend the effort to find the receipt(s)
    and pays the receivable.   Most lender errors are caught when the SCB reviews
    the claim prior to the actual payment. The PQR process identifies a low percentage
    of improper payments due to human error.  As discussed previously, claims may
    have been paid properly but documentation may be missing.  Occasionally the servicer
    is unable to provide origination documentation because they did not obtain it
    during the servicing transfer.  As part of the audit, the lender is required to
    send it and must return the claim payment if the agency doesn’t have sufficient
    documentation on file to confirm the borrower qualified for the loan.'
