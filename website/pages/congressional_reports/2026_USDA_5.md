SurveyData:
- Answer: 'Overpayments are identified through RMA’s Compliance Activities and Results
    System (CARS) in accordance with Standard Reinsurance Agreement requirements.
    RMA utilizes the results of a variety of audits and reviews to compile data, conduct
    complex survey analytics, identify root causes, and determine the amount of improper
    payments.  The data collected consists of but is not limited to; the type of error(s)
    that led to the improper payment, the stage at which the payment became improper,
    and who was responsible for the payment becoming improper.  This comprehensive
    analysis allows RMA to identify the root cause of improper payments and develop
    an effective corrective action plan.

    For the Rural Housing Service (RHS), improper payments are identified through
    supervisory visits.  Documentation is reviewed for accuracy and changes in income
    levels and individuals living in the residence.  If is identified that a tenent
    was overpaid, they are advised and a receivable is established in the accounting
    system. Conditions that give rise to improper payments are if a tenant misstated
    their income or how many people are living in the unit or if the owner did not
    update the paper work to indicate that the unit was vacant.   The supervisory
    visits, plus verifying wage match by state help catch and correct misstatements.


    The SFH-Claims Branch, Lender Quality Section performs loss claim post payment
    quality reviews (PQR) on lenders with the highest volume of loss claims processed
    during specified timeframes based on a statistical sampling.  The complete file
    including the origination package is reviewed for compliance.  When lender errors
    are identified during the review, an initial findings letter is sent out, whereby
    the lender has an opportunity to provide additional documentation to dispute the
    findings.  Once a final determination is made, a final findings letter is sent
    out and a receivable is set up to collect the funds.  It must be noted that not
    all findings are errors.  The agency requests claim funds back if it is determined
    that adequate documentation was not provided.  Often, if the finding is minimal,
    the lender determines they don’t want to expend the effort to find the receipt(s)
    and pays the receivable.   Most lender errors are caught when the SCB reviews
    the claim prior to the actual payment.  The PQR process identifies a low percentage
    of improper payments due to human error.  As discussed previously, claims may
    have been paid properly but documentation may be missing.  Occasionally the servicer
    is unable to provide origination documentation because they did not obtain it
    during the servicing transfer.  As part of the audit, the lender is required to
    send it and must return the claim payment if the agency doesn’t have sufficient
    documentation on file to confirm the borrower qualified for the loan.'
