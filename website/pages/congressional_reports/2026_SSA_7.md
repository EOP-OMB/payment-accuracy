- Answer: 'We hold managers, program officials, and senior executives accountable
    for reducing improper payments. These employees’ annual performance plans reflect
    their responsibility to support efforts to maintain sufficient internal controls
    to prevent, detect, and recover improper payments and meet targets to reduce improper
    payments. In FY 2025, we revised the Manages Performance element to incorporate
    “Holding Employees Accountable” as a requirement for all managers and supervisors.
    To achieve a successful performance rating, managers and supervisors must now
    meet this standard.


    Senior Executive performance plans must clearly align with organizational goals
    and objectives under the Results Driven Critical Element, and the performance
    levels in the performance requirements must reflect agency targets.


    In fiscal year 2019, we established an Improper Payments Prevention Team, now
    referred to as the Improper Payments team. The team works with key agency stakeholders
    to monitor and report externally on corrective actions taken or planned to mitigate
    the root causes of improper payments. The team works closely with the Chief-level
    Improper Payments Oversight Board (IPOB), who is responsible for monitoring and
    driving improper payment initiatives. Beginning in December 2025, the IPOB increased
    their meeting frequency from quarterly to monthly. The Chief Risk Officer or their
    designee serves as the executive chair for the IPOB. Additionally, we established
    a new SSI Improvement team to ensure comprehensive and dedicated oversight of
    the SSI program and to advance key reforms in policies, processes, and automation.
    The SSI Improvement team reports on their progress and plans during the IPOB meetings.'
