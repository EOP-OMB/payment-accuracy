- Answer: 'The most recent Office of the Inspector General (OIG) Payment Integrity
    Information Act of 2019 (PIIA) annual compliance audit was for fiscal year (FY)
    2025. In FY 2025, we had three programs reporting estimates above the statutory
    threshold, our Old-Age and Survivors Insurance (OASI), Disability Insurance (DI),
    and Supplemental Security Income (SSI) programs. In the FY 2025 PIIA compliance
    audit, the Social Security Administration’s (SSA) OIG concluded the agency was
    compliant with all 10 reporting requirements for the OASI and DI programs. OIG
    determined that for the SSI program, the agency did not (1) demonstrate improvements
    to payment integrity or reach a tolerable improper payment and unknown payment
    rate and (2) did not report an improper payment and unknown payment estimate of
    less than 10 percent.


    In the FY 2025 PIIA compliance audit report, OIG encouraged SSA to continue working
    to implement open recommendations from prior audits, which included (1) conducting
    a study to expand Access to Financial Institutions (AFI) searches between the
    SSI initial application and subsequent eligibility redeterminations and (2) completing
    the Foreign Travel Data (FTD) exchange project.


    SSI Non-Compliance:

    For the SSI program, of the estimated $7.0 billion in SSI overpayments, approximately
    $6.5 billion were due to factors outside of the agency’s control because the data
    or information to validate payment accuracy prior to making payment does not exist
    or is inaccessible to us. Failure to report changes is the primary cause of both
    overpayments and underpayments.


    The majority of SSI improper payments are a result of unreported or untimely reporting
    of financial accounts, wages, and in-kind support and maintenance (ISM). To ensure
    individuals continue to meet all statutory and regulatory requirements to be eligible
    and to receive the correct SSI payment amount, we conduct annual SSI non-medical
    redeterminations (RZ) and limited issues (LI). To ensure the most cost-effective
    investment of agency resources, we use a predictive model to estimate the likelihood
    and magnitude of improper payments to select cases for discretionary RZs. In FY
    2026, we processed over 2.6 million RZs and LIs, and in FY 2027, we plan to process
    2.9 million RZs and LIs. We issued guidance and reminders to technicians to assist
    with RZ/LI processing throughout the fiscal year. We also placed greater emphasis
    on completing priority LIs to ensure we are maximizing impact on improper payments.


    We routinely identify unreported financial accounts, wages, and ISM through the
    RZ and LI processes and also attempt to do so through the other mechanisms described
    below.


    Financial Accounts - AFI verifies bank account information and detects undisclosed
    bank accounts with participating financial institutions. AFI has been used for
    SSI initial claims, pre-effectuation review contacts, and RZs, and when there
    is a resource-related diary present on an RZ or LI. In August 2025, we implemented
    a zero-dollar AFI tolerance for SSI aged 65+ initial claim allowances before adjudicating
    to payment. In May 2026, we expanded this change to all SSI initial claim allowances,
    including initial claim reopenings, and appeal reversals establishing initial
    eligibility. This strategy mandates that bank account verification is fully completed
    prior to adjudication and before payments are issued, thereby eliminating improper
    payments as early as possible rather than pursuing recovery after we issue payments.
    In September 2026, we implemented a new systems alert to ensure we are conducting
    AFI reviews, as required by policy, in association with five SSI resource-related
    diaries. We created the AFI Resource Center and added it to the National Workload
    Resource Center to support technicians in efficiently processing AFI. The AFI
    Resource Center contains pertinent policy references, systems instructions, user
    guides, and training resources. It also contains a Financial Institution Average
    Response Time report and instructions on expediting claims processing when there
    are slow response times from financial institutions. In FY 2027, we plan to implement
    a new LI diary that will identify cases from a batch run that have been flagged
    for potential ineligibility.


    Wages - We developed a new wage reporting option where we receive wage and employment
    information from a commercial payroll data provider (PDP) through an automated
    payroll information exchange. In February 2026, we released emails to a targeted
    population to notify non-participating beneficiaries about PIE to increase participation.
    We plan to implement several key enhancements to PIE, including the ability to
    request historical wage data and a process for handling Social Security Numbers
    flagged for name mismatch. Additionally, we will develop more robust management
    information to enable more detailed analysis and guide further improvements to
    address pain points.


    ISM - FY 2025 was the first year of implementation of three new regulations relating
    to ISM that were intended, in part, to reduce the ISM-reporting burden on SSI
    applicants and recipients. On September 30, 2024, we implemented changes that
    included: (1)  omitting the value of food from our ISM calculations (which, in
    part, eliminated the requirement to provide detailed information about the value
    of food ISM provided to an SSI applicant or recipient); (2) expanding the definition
    of a “public assistance household” by adding Supplemental Nutrition Assistance
    Program to the list of public income-maintenance (PIM) programs and by requiring
    the receipt of a PIM payment for only one additional household member (other than
    the SSI applicant or recipient) instead of requiring the receipt of a PIM payment
    for every other member of the household; and (3) expanding the rental subsidy
    exception nationwide to simplify our ISM rules and ensure uniform application
    of the policy.


    In FY 2025, we transitioned field office phone systems to the same telephone platform
    used by the teleservice centers. By using the same telephone platform, we are
    able to route reports of SSI recipient address and living arrangement changes
    directly to a field office representative. Field office representatives are able
    to update the SSI recipients’ record with the change of address and develop living
    arrangement or ISM updates that could impact their payment amounts. Previously,
    teleservice center representatives had to refer these reports to a field office
    for development at a later date and in some instances, the field office would
    have to recontact the claimant. In June 2026, we further refined this business
    process by routing change of address calls to field office employees who are specialized
    in developing SSI living arrangement and ISM changes. We also added language to
    our policy to clarify the instructions for determining ISM in non-institutional
    care situations and issued ISM and living arrangement reminders to technicians.


    FTD - The agency suspends SSI payments to recipients— United States (U.S.) citizens
    and non-U.S. citizens—who are outside the United States for an entire calendar
    month. Individuals outside the U.S. for 30 consecutive days become ineligible
    for SSI payments until they return and remain present in the U.S. for 30 consecutive
    days. Payments resume after this period.  FTD is an agency application with direct
    access to the Department of Homeland Security’s (DHS) Arrival and Departure Information
    System (ADIS) with travel information that, if the agency maximizes its use, will
    further uncover unreported U.S. absences that affect SSI eligibility; thus, increasing
    the detection of improper payments. Effective January 2026, we expanded the use
    of FTD to U.S. citizens during RZs and initial claims allowances, prior to the
    issuance of SSI payments, in certain situations. The expansion also applies to
    non-U.S. citizens during initial claims allowances, prior to the issuance of SSI
    payments, in all cases. In FY 2026, we engaged with DHS’s Customs and Border Protection
    to discuss expanding the use of ADIS to include a batch process. We plan to implement
    the automation of bulk requests in FY 2027.


    We have several broad-based initiatives in place or underway that are designed
    to ensure technician compliance with policy and improve payment accuracy.


    In September 2025, the Commissioner established a new SSI Improvement team to
    ensure comprehensive and dedicated oversight of the SSI program and to transform
    the service we provide to SSI recipients. Since its inception, the team has revised
    and clarified a number of SSI policies, prioritized work that will drive down
    improper payments, and improved the program’s digital footprint.


    In September 2024, we released the 21st Century PolicyNet (21CPN), which replaced
    PolicyNet, the 20+ year-old software platform that contains multiple applications
    and hosts thousands of pages of policy instruction. In FYs 2025-2026, we continued
    development and refinement of the system to expand functionality and improve user
    experience. We also launched a Policy Assistant Tool (PAT). PAT is a chatbot developed
    to support policy navigation and operational efficiency including reducing improper
    payments. PAT’s enterprise-wide release via the 21CPN platform occurred in May
    2026. Expansion plans are underway to broaden access to advanced retrieval capabilities,
    support enterprise-wide integration, and deliver targeted enhancements based on
    user feedback. Ongoing efforts prioritize user-driven enhancements to further
    strengthen PAT’s value and support future innovation across the agency.


    We continue development of the Consolidated Claims Experience (CCE), a modernized
    user-friendly web-based application designed to centralize and streamline benefit
    claim processing actions, reduce training time for technicians, and provide a
    more intuitive approach for data collection and processing of benefit claims.
    In FY 2026, we released various enhancements to CCE SSI. In FY 2027, we will continue
    to migrate functionality into the modern environment.


    Upload Documents and eSignature services allow beneficiaries, recipients, and
    individual representative payees to submit and sign documents electronically based
    on a technician request. We also offer a customer-initiated option allowing them
    to electronically submit certain forms and evidence to the agency without technician
    initiation. In FY 2026, through frontline training, we encouraged field office
    technicians to promote the use of Upload Documents to our customers to help avoid
    processing delays and reduce administrative burden. We also continued to webify
    forms for a mobile friendly experience and increase the number of forms. In July
    2026, we launched the first batch of forms utilizing a new conversational approach
    in Upload Documents. The new approach presents form items in a more easily understood,
    conversational style making them accessible to customers of all education and
    reading levels. Similar to previously introduced dynamic webforms, conversational
    forms will not necessarily ask questions in a linear sequence that mirrors the
    paper form. Instead, customer responses will determine which additional questions
    appear. Although form questions will be presented in a new conversational manner,
    a Portable Document Format (PDF), identical to the paper and fillable PDF versions
    of the form, will still be generated. Customers will have an opportunity to preview
    the PDF and edit any responses prior to submission.'

- Answer: 'We hold managers, program officials, and senior executives accountable
    for reducing improper payments. These employees’ annual performance plans reflect
    their responsibility to support efforts to maintain sufficient internal controls
    to prevent, detect, and recover improper payments and meet targets to reduce improper
    payments. In FY 2025, we revised the Manages Performance element to incorporate
    “Holding Employees Accountable” as a requirement for all managers and supervisors.
    To achieve a successful performance rating, managers and supervisors must now
    meet this standard.


    Senior Executive performance plans must clearly align with organizational goals
    and objectives under the Results Driven Critical Element, and the performance
    levels in the performance requirements must reflect agency targets.


    In fiscal year 2019, we established an Improper Payments Prevention Team, now
    referred to as the Improper Payments team. The team works with key agency stakeholders
    to monitor and report externally on corrective actions taken or planned to mitigate
    the root causes of improper payments. The team works closely with the Chief-level
    Improper Payments Oversight Board (IPOB), who is responsible for monitoring and
    driving improper payment initiatives. Beginning in December 2025, the IPOB increased
    their meeting frequency from quarterly to monthly. The Chief Risk Officer or their
    designee serves as the executive chair for the IPOB. Additionally, we established
    a new SSI Improvement team to ensure comprehensive and dedicated oversight of
    the SSI program and to transform the service we provide to SSI recipients. The
    SSI Improvement team reports on their progress and plans during the IPOB meetings.'
