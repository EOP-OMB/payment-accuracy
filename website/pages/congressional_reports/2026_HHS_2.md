    Answer: "HHS developed effective corrective actions and mitigation strategies\
      \ that are focused on identified root causes for improper payments and plans\
      \ to continue these actions in FY 2027.  The majority of overpayments were due\
      \ to manual errors. Corrective actions focused on staff training, audits, and\
      \ risk models to address the root causes of improper payments in FY 2027. In\
      \ addition, HHS also plans to use identifying and remediating system defects\
      \ as a mitigation strategy. \n\nAdditionally, Duplicate SSN errors made up a\
      \ significant portion of overpayments. To address these in July 2026, CMS extended\
      \ rules already in place for EDE agent/broker applications to enforce restrictions\
      \ on agent/broker assisted applications through other channels to ensure that\
      \ verifiable SSNs or immigration information must be provided on applications\
      \ with an agent/broker including through consumer applications on HealthCare.gov\
      \ and the Marketplace Call Center. Such applications are now required to include\
      \ SSNs verified through the Social Security Administration (SSA) for all individuals\
      \ over 90 days old who are requesting coverage (“applicants”). Through all application\
      \ channels, Agents have always been required to provide SSNs for applicants\
      \ who have them, but this system update will help CMS better enforce the requirement\
      \ systematically for all channels where agents and brokers can assist with applications,\
      \ including consumer applications on HealthCare.gov and when calling the Marketplace\
      \ Call Center. For any noncitizen applicants who do not have an SSN, information\
      \ from immigration documents that can be verified through the Department of\
      \ Homeland Security in real time will be required for application submission.\
      \ Because for an SSN to be verified by SSA it must match SSA records for the\
      \ applicants name and date of birth, we believe this change is likely to reduce\
      \ the rate of duplicate SSNs. In addition, we’ve made some additional specific\
      \ technical changes to our process for canceling what appear to be overlapping\
      \ enrollments based on matching demographic information. We believe these changes\
      \ will also reduce the occurrence of duplicate SSNs across policies."

    Answer: "HHS planned and implemented a series of corrective actions to address\
      \ the root causes of improper payments and unknown payments in the FY 2026 Child\
      \ Care and Development Fund (CCDF) program. CCDF did not have any unknown payments\
      \ in FY 2026; therefore, the corrective actions described below apply to improper\
      \ payments only. \n\nBecause CCDF is administered by states, territories, and\
      \ tribes, HHS focused its corrective actions on strengthening Lead Agency program\
      \ integrity and accountability and enhancing federal oversight practices. \n\
      Following the RY25 error rate reporting results, HHS conducted training on the\
      \ CCDF improper payments methodology, provided technical assistance on program\
      \ integrity and accountability, and held federal-state joint case reviews to\
      \ provide oversight of state implementation of case reviews. These actions were\
      \ implemented during FY 2026 and will continue into FY 2027 as part of ongoing\
      \ oversight and technical assistance efforts. \n\nIn FY 2026, HHS also expanded\
      \ training and technical assistance activities through targeted program integrity\
      \ initiatives. In July 2026, HHS hosted a program integrity series at a national\
      \ meeting for CCDF state and territory administrators to reinforce key program\
      \ integrity expectations. Sessions covered CCDF program integrity fundamentals,\
      \ internal controls and risk management, fraud prevention and investigations,\
      \ and using CCDF error rate review results to support program improvement. In\
      \ 2026 Q4, HHS launched national and regional Program Integrity and Accountability\
      \ Peer Learning Groups to provide states with an ongoing forum to share strategies,\
      \ identify common challenges, and collaborate with peers on program integrity\
      \ solutions. These actions help states build stronger oversight systems, identify\
      \ risks, increase fraud prevention and deterrence, and take corrective action\
      \ to ensure CCDF funds are used appropriately and effectively.\n\nHHS also continued\
      \ to require corrective action plans (CAPs) for states with improper payment\
      \ rates above the established 10 percent threshold. During FY 2026, HHS provided\
      \ ongoing technical assistance and oversight to support state CAP implementation,\
      \ including regular CAP progress check-in meetings with states. These activities\
      \ helped states identify and address the root causes of improper payments, strengthen\
      \ eligibility and documentation practices, identify technical assistance needs,\
      \ including updating or enhancing data systems to prevent errors, and improve\
      \ oversight of program operations. HHS will continue CAP-related technical assistance\
      \ and oversight efforts in FY 2027 for states that exceed the 10 percent threshold,\
      \ including implementing additional accountability measures for states that\
      \ continue to exceed the threshold. \n\nIn addition, HHS is developing revisions\
      \ to the current improper payments reporting methodology to require more frequent\
      \ error rate reporting, moving from the current triennial cycle toward a biennial\
      \ or annual reporting cycle. These methodology revisions are planned for continued\
      \ development in FY 2027. The goal of this action is to improve the timeliness\
      \ of improper payment information, identify program integrity risks sooner,\
      \ support faster corrective action, improve accountability for states with high\
      \ improper payment rates, and reduce future improper payments. As part of this\
      \ effort, HHS will also consider opportunities to streamline reporting processes,\
      \ clarify requirements, and reduce unnecessary state burden while maintaining\
      \ strong oversight and accountability."

- Answer: Improper payments identified through recovery audits typically arise from
    insufficient or missing documentation to support claims, inaccurate billing, unsupported
    diagnosis codes submitted for risk-adjusted payment, incorrect eligibility determinations,
    and errors in coordinating benefits with other insurers. In FY 2026, Medicare
    Fee-for-Service recovery auditors identified overpayments primarily related to
    documentation deficiencies in hospital outpatient, skilled nursing facility, laboratory
    test, and hospice claims, and the Medicare Secondary Payer Recovery Auditor identified
    overpayments related to coordination of benefits errors. HHS resolves the conditions
    giving rise to these improper payments through targeted corrective actions, including
    enhanced provider education, prior authorization requirements for high-risk service
    types, expanded use of the Fraud Defense Operations Center to identify suspect
    billing patterns before and after payment, and Risk Adjustment Data Validation
    audits to address unsupported diagnoses submitted by Medicare Advantage Organizations.
    To recover overpayments, HHS and its contractors employ several methods, including
    issuing demand letters, offsetting future payments, requiring direct refunds from
    providers and plan sponsors, and using Treasury's debt collection and offset processes
    when debts remain unresolved. Medicaid, the Children's Health Insurance Program,
    and ACF programs recover disallowed costs through federal-state reconciliation
    processes, direct recoupments informed by Payment Error Rate Measurement reviews
    and program-specific eligibility audits, and the administrative disallowance process
    for grant-based programs, with HHS working closely with states and grantees to
    address the underlying conditions driving errors and prevent recurrence.
