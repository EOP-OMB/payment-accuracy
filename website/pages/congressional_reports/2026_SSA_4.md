    Accountability: 'We are committed to being good stewards of taxpayer dollars and
      ensuring the public has confidence that we manage their tax dollars wisely.
      We demonstrate a commitment to sound management practices. To ensure stewardship
      and the efficient administration of our programs, we have established performance
      measures in our Annual Performance Plan for fiscal years (FY) 2026–2027 to track
      our progress. Under strategic goal 2, “Preserve and Protect Public Resources,”
      there are three performance measures directly related to reduction of improper
      payments: Supplemental Security Income program overpayment accuracy; Old-Age
      and Survivors Insurance program overpayment accuracy, and Disability Insurance
      program overpayment accuracy.


      We hold managers, program officials, and senior executives accountable for reducing
      improper payments. These employees’ annual performance plans reflect their responsibility
      to support efforts to maintain sufficient internal controls to prevent, detect,
      and recover improper payments and meet targets to reduce improper payments.
      In FY 2025, we revised the Manages Performance element to incorporate “Holding
      Employees Accountable” as a requirement for all managers and supervisors. To
      achieve a successful performance rating, managers and supervisors must now meet
      this standard.


      We assess managers throughout the agency on these standards each year and hold
      them accountable for meeting improper payment reduction targets. Each agency
      department adds expectations that are more detailed for their positions describing
      what is expected for meeting these performance standards.


      If management or employees do not meet established performance expectations,
      they may be placed on an Opportunity to Perform Successfully. Failure to demonstrate
      improvement may result in an unsuccessful performance rating, which could lead
      to demotion or removal from Federal service.


      Senior Executive performance plans must clearly align with organizational goals
      and objectives under the Results Driven Critical Element. Performance levels
      in the performance requirements must reflect agency targets.


      The Improper Payments Oversight Board (IPOB), consisting of senior executive
      membership, ensures we are focusing on improper payment prevention, formulating
      clear and innovative strategies, and driving timely results agency-wide. The
      Chief Risk Officer or their designee serves as the executive chair for the IPOB.
      In FY 2019, we established an Improper Payments Prevention Team, now known as
      the Improper Payments team. The Improper Payments team works closely with the
      IPOB and monitors corrective actions taken or planned to mitigate the root causes
      of improper payments.

      Act.


      The effective internal controls we incorporate into our business processes and
      financial management systems, as well as the program integrity efforts, support
      our Commissioner’s annual assurance statement to the President and Congress.

    Description: 'Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) is the performance of significant
      physical or mental activities in work for pay or profit, or in work of a type
      generally performed for pay or profit, regardless of the legality of the work.
      Beneficiaries are required to report changes in their work activity and increases
      in earnings to ensure proper payments and continued eligibility for benefits.
      Improper payments based on SGA are the leading cause of improper payments in
      the Disability Insurance (DI) program based on a 5-year annual average of $1.2
      billion; most occurring as a result of beneficiaries or representative payees
      failing to report changes in work.


      Through the process known as the payroll information exchange (PIE), we obtain
      wage and employment information from a commercial payroll data provider for
      individuals who have provided authorization and have participating employers.
      In February 2026, we released emails to a targeted population to notify non-participating
      beneficiaries about PIE to increase participation. We plan to implement several
      key enhancements to PIE, including the ability to request historical wage data
      and a process for handling Social Security Numbers flagged for name mismatch.
      Additionally, we will develop more robust management information to enable more
      detailed analysis and guide further improvements to address pain points.


      PIE will help improve payment accuracy and reduce the reporting burden on individuals
      when they authorize us to obtain this information through an information exchange,
      and we receive it. Implementation of PIE will result in more efficient use of
      our administrative resources because our technicians will spend less time manually
      requesting this information from SSA authorized wage verification companies
      and employers, manually entering data into our systems from an individual’s
      pay records, contacting individuals, and assisting individuals with the results
      of incomplete or untimely reporting.


      For individuals or employers not participating in PIE, we continue to offer
      multiple reporting options such as the myWageReport online tool, SSA Mobile
      Wage Reporting, by mail or in-office visit. In FY 2026, we continued to use
      our social media channels to post wage reporting reminders. We aired information
      on wage reporting reminders on televisions in field office reception areas and
      will continue to do so in FY 2027. We are also adding a reminder in the 2027
      Cost of Living Adjustment notices about work-related reporting requirements.


      We are developing the Electronic Work Continuing Disability Review (eWCDR) application
      to replace and modernize eWork, the system technicians use to process CDRs based
      on work activity. The new system will enforce policy and best practices with
      an intuitive user interface and eliminate current system limitations that lead
      to large improper payments through delays and processing errors. The application
      will streamline the disability review process and have a positive impact on
      reducing SGA improper payments. In November 2026, we will eliminate a systems
      limitation by creating a bridge between the eWCDR backend program and legacy
      eWork application. Technicians will still process work CDRs through eWork, but
      we will release the backend processing that will resolve improper payments based
      on processing limitations. In December 2026, we will release the eWCDR application
      to users and retire eWork.


      We use WorkSmart to identify DI beneficiaries whose earnings may place them
      at risk of being overpaid. We created WorkSmart to reduce improper payments
      by alerting cases quickly after a beneficiary starts work. In FY 2026, WorkSmart
      continued to alert cases for work CDRs based on available data. In addition,
      WorkSmart uses PIE data to identify and alert cases that may require a work
      CDR. These actions will continue into FY 2027.

      We complete work CDR quality reviews to ensure compliance with policy and the
      Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015 by evaluating a stratified random sample of processed
      work CDRs monthly. This review captures processing times, assesses Trust Fund
      impact from policy non-compliance, measures accuracy rates, identifies procedural
      and training improvements, and collects data on new processing procedures. We
      provide case-level feedback and share the broader data analysis with stakeholders.
      We completed data analysis of FY 2023 and FY 2024 reviews and published them
      in December 2025. We released the report of the FY 2025 reviews in September
      2026. We began the FY 2026 reviews and anticipate releasing the report in FY
      2027.


      We implemented a change to allow employment data from the Detailed Earnings
      Query and the National Database of New Hires to propagate to form SSA-821, Work
      Activity Report, saving technicians an average of four minutes per form. Additionally,
      to improve understanding and reduce the burden on our customers, we revised

      why we are asking for information and how work incentives may reduce countable

      SSA-820 publication occurred in February 2026.


      In January 2026, we released a reminder to technicians to inform beneficiaries

      In March 2026, Policy experts presented on Section 301 processing at an Area

      Computational errors are the second leading cause of DI improper payments with
      a 5-year annual average of $242.4 million. These errors occur due to many factors
      including systems limitations, manual calculation of benefits involving complicated
      policies, technician errors, incorrectly processed or unprocessed earnings adjustments,
      beneficiaries not reporting complete and pertinent information, and lack of
      accurate and accessible data.


      Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) makes up $3.8 million of the $242.4 million
      of the 5-year annual average in DI computational errors. WEP reduced or offset
      Social Security monthly benefits to beneficiaries who received a pension based
      on non-covered earnings. Most of these workers had non-covered earnings from
      federal, state, or local governments. We had previously relied on applicants,
      beneficiaries, or their representative payees to report their entitlement to
      current or future non-covered pensions; however, they did not always report
      the receipt of or changes to a pension, resulting in benefit calculation and
      payment errors. WEP errors also occurred when the agency did not correctly impose
      reductions or offset, misapplied exceptions or exemptions, or applied WEP but
      not the Government Pension Offset (or vice versa) for dually entitled beneficiaries.
      On January 5, 2025, the Social Security Fairness Act of 2023 was signed into
      law, thereby repealing WEP. December 2023 is the last month that WEP applied.
      This means that those rules no longer applied to benefits payable for January
      2024 and later. We anticipate the repeal will significantly reduce and eventually
      eliminate associated improper payments in future years.


      To address other computational issues within the agency’s control, we developed
      several tools. In January 2026, we released a new Payment Preview Tool for Processing
      Center (PC) users. The tool assists in previewing manual inputs prior to processing.
      In July 2026, we released the Family Portrait tool, which retrieves various
      data points, summarizes entitlements by beneficiary or by record, alerts technicians
      of other records that beneficiaries are entitled on, and shows Primary Insurance
      Amount (PIA) data on each record. By providing a comprehensive overview, the
      tool helps prevent missed combined family maximums, as well as family maximums
      not being applied or terminated correctly. The tool also integrates with Parisi
      Rule worksheets, which minimizes manual inputs and reduces potential errors.


      When the disability maximum payment and the PIA are equal, any auxiliaries on
      the record will have a zero monthly benefit amount (technical entitlement only).
      In April 2026, we added a systems alert to identify when this situation exists
      to remind technicians to review all beneficiary identification codes on the
      record.


      We create automated “robotic” programs that perform routine or repetitive tasks
      and increase the speed and accuracy of manual processing. Bots are available
      to PC technicians to assist with processing manual awards or post-entitlement
      actions. In FY 2026, we developed three new bots with two impacting both OASI
      and DI. First, the No Offset bot, released in June 2026, processes the release
      of past-due DI benefits when no SSI offset is present. Second, the Death Reclamation
      bot, released in July 2026, processes reclamations received from Treasury for
      OASDI benefit payments in the termination month due to death. Lastly, when we
      are notified of a bankruptcy, an automatic stay of collection is triggered.
      We plan to release the bankruptcy bot in early FY 2027 to stop overpayment collection
      immediately. We are committed to making long-term investments in robotics technology
      to improve business processes and eliminate manual actions.


      Workers compensation (WC) and public disability benefit (PDB) errors are the
      third leading cause of DI improper payments with a 5-year annual average of
      $166.0 million. To address these errors, in April 2026, we streamlined the policy
      for verification of WC/PDB proof in the PCs. We updated the timeframe of acceptable
      WC/PDB proof of periodic payments, specifically that WC/PDB proof/evidence is
      considered current if it is no more than one year old. We are requiring technicians
      to obtain proof/verification of WC/PDB payments concurrently from all appropriate
      sources after attempting contact with the number holder. We also updated the
      policy on control measures, such as establishing diaries, to avoid delays in
      the development of WC/PDB proof.


      We continue working on an exchange with the Department of Labor (DOL) relating
      to Federal Employee Compensation Act data (FECA). FECA provides income and medical
      cost protection to covered Federal civilian employees injured on the job, employees
      who have incurred a work-related injury or occupational disease, and beneficiaries
      of employees whose death is attributable to a job-related injury or occupational
      disease. FECA is administered by the DOL, and receipt of FECA benefits can offset
      DI benefits. In December 2023, we established a Memorandum of Understanding
      with DOL for use of an employee compensation portal. Technicians can submit
      individual real-time queries in the portal to obtain FECA data and complete
      computations. We continue to explore data exchange opportunities that would
      improve efficiency and accuracy of related computations by eliminating the need
      for a manual query and transition to a batch process. We are working to establish
      a Computer Matching Agreement to enable DOL and SSA in FY 2027 to automate systems
      for record comparison, thereby reducing SSA’s reliance on self-reporting.

    Answer: 'Improper payments within the agency’s control are caused by our failure
      to access the data or information needed to make accurate payments, or we took
      an incorrect action. An incorrect action could be due to incorrect policy interpretation
      and application, mathematical error, typographical error, or delay in input.
      To prevent improper payments from occurring, we provide training and reminders
      for technicians and develop automation solutions to improve accuracy.


      Improper payments outside the agency’s control occur when we are unable to access
      the data needed to calculate an accurate payment because the beneficiary or
      a representative payee did not provide the requested information or provided
      inaccurate information necessary to compute the accurate benefit amount. It
      occurs because of beneficiaries’ or representative payees’ failure to report
      changes that impact benefit eligibility and/or payment amounts. We explicitly
      inform individuals of their reporting requirements during the application process
      and again within award letters, pamphlets periodically sent to beneficiaries,
      and in standard language in most official agency correspondence. However, beneficiaries
      and representative payees still may fail to report these changes or may not
      report changes in time to prevent an improper payment.

    Description: 'Computational errors are the leading cause of Old-Age and Survivors
      Insurance (OASI) improper payments with a 5-year annual average of $1.4 billion.
      These errors occur due to many factors including systems limitations, manual
      calculation of benefits involving complicated policies, technician errors, incorrectly
      processed or unprocessed earnings adjustments, beneficiaries not reporting complete
      and pertinent information, and lack of accurate and accessible data.


      Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) makes up $315.6 million of the $1.4 billion
      5-year annual average in OASI computational errors. Separately, errors relating
      to Government Pension Offset (GPO) resulted in a 5-year annual average of $72.8
      million in improper payments. WEP and GPO reduced or offset Social Security
      monthly benefits to beneficiaries who received a pension based on non-covered
      earnings. Most of these workers had non-covered earnings from federal, state,
      or local governments. We had previously relied on applicants, beneficiaries,
      or their representative payees to report their entitlement to current or future
      non-covered pensions; however, they did not always report the receipt of or
      changes to a pension, resulting in benefit calculation and payment errors. WEP
      and GPO errors also occurred when the agency did not correctly impose reductions
      or offset, misapplied exceptions or exemptions, or applied WEP but not GPO (or
      vice versa) for dually entitled beneficiaries. On January 5, 2025, the Social
      Security Fairness Act of 2023 was signed into law, thereby repealing WEP/GPO.
      December 2023 is the last month that WEP and GPO applied. This means that those
      rules no longer applied to benefits payable for January 2024 and later. We anticipate
      the WEP/GPO repeal will significantly reduce and eventually eliminate associated
      improper payments in future years.


      To address other computational issues within the agency’s control, we developed

      Center (PC) users. The tool assists in previewing manual inputs prior to processing.

      Rule worksheets, which minimizes manual inputs and reduces potential errors.

      recipient’s or couple’s non-medical eligibility factors (resources, income,
      and living arrangements). To ensure the most cost-effective investment of agency
      resources, we use a predictive model to estimate the likelihood and magnitude
      of improper payments to select cases for discretionary RZs. In addition to our
      modeling process, we also select cases for RZs based on selected case characteristics.
      The RZ process also selects limited issue (LI) reviews, which are reviews of
      a specific issue or event related to a recipient’s or couple’s non-medical SSI
      eligibility factors. In FY 2026, we processed over 2.6 million RZs and LIs,
      and in FY 2027, we plan to process 2.9 million RZs and LIs. We issued guidance

    Answer: 'Improper payments within the agency’s control are caused by our failure
      to access the data or information needed to make accurate payments, or we took
      an incorrect action. An incorrect action could be due to incorrect policy interpretation
      and application, mathematical error, typographical error, or delay in input.
      To prevent improper payments from occurring, we provide training and reminders
      for technicians and develop automation solutions to improve accuracy.


      Improper payments outside the agency’s control occur when the data or information
      needed does not exist or because of our inability to access the data or information
      needed. These improper payments occur because there is no database or dataset
      that currently exists where we can check eligibility prior to making the Supplemental
      Security Income (SSI) payment. For example, the law requires us to evaluate
      an individual’s income, which includes support and maintenance provided in-kind.
      “In-kind” income is difficult to accurately value because it can fluctuate each
      month as household expenses, composition, and the type of assistance provided
      may change. Additionally, there is no database or data source we can use to
      verify these factors. We rely on recipients to report changes in household expenses,
      composition, and contributions accurately and timely.


      Improper payments outside the agency’s control also occur when we are unable
      to access the data needed to calculate an accurate payment because the recipient
      or a representative payee did not provide the requested information or provided
      inaccurate information necessary to compute the accurate benefit amount. It
      occurs because of recipients’ or representative payees’ failure to report changes
      in any of their eligibility factors (e.g., an increase in the value of their
      resources or a change in their wages). Failure to report changes is the primary
      cause of both overpayments and underpayments. We explicitly inform all individuals
      of their reporting requirements during the application process and again within
      every award letter, pamphlets periodically sent to recipients, and in standard
      language in most official agency correspondence. However, recipients and representative
      payees still may fail to report these changes or may not report changes in time
      to prevent an improper payment.'
