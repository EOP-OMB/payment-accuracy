    The largest category of errors, accounting for $16.2 million, was Unknown Payments
    (UP), where missing or conflicting information made it impossible to determine
    payment validity. Key drivers included foundational data contradictions on vouchers,
    such as a traveler’s duty station and temporary duty location being listed as
    the same city and missing Invitational Travel Authorizations (ITAs) for non-medical
    attendants. Technically Improper Payments constituted the next largest category,
    with $10.6 million in errors. These were largely due to process non-compliance
    and a lack of traveler understanding regarding proper documentation. Common scenarios
    included the submission of un-ticketed flight itineraries or rental agreements
    instead of final, paid receipts, and the retroactive approval of travel authorizations
    without required justifications.


    Monetary Losses to the Agency (MLA), totaling $7.5 million, were significantly
    driven by a systemic issue where DTS lacks the functionality to process unique
    entitlements for civilian evacuations, leading to substantial overpayments when
    travelers improperly use the system for these claims. Additional root causes identified
    across the agencies include inadequate internal controls and segregation of duties,
    inconsistent application of travel regulations, and a failure by approving officials
    to conduct sufficiently detailed reviews to identify and correct errors before
    payment. Collectively, these conditions were compounded by weak internal controls,
    inadequate segregation of duties, inconsistent application of travel regulations,
