    evaluations and performance. Under the new Risk Management and Internal Control

    Weakness. Command leadership must then report this deficiency quarterly to HQDA
    and, most importantly, address it in their annual Statement of Assurance—a key
    component of their performance evaluation. This ensures executive managers are
    directly accountable for the internal control failures within their organizations.


    To change behavior at the transaction level, the plan implements clear ramifications
    for continued non-compliance by Approving and Certifying Officers. The Army is
    developing automated procedures to systematically identify officials and travelers
    associated with improper payments. Once flagged, these individuals will face mandatory
    remedial actions, which range from targeted refresher training and supervisor
    notifications for initial infractions to the temporary suspension or permanent
    removal of their DTS approval authority for repeat offenses. This tiered enforcement
    strategy ensures that the consequences are proportional to the failure and provides
    a direct, personal incentive for Certifying Officers to adhere to verification
    policies, thereby preventing the errors that lead to improper and unknown payments.

    of the Law and the President’s Policies,” reinforces executive accountability

  detail_IP_Causes: 'The primary cause of improper payments in the Army''s DTS program
    is a significant monetary loss, totaling over $116 million year-to-date, stemming
    from failures to validate entitlements. These are not simply procedural errors
    but actual financial losses to the government. The top drivers of these monetary
    losses are overpayments for travel at the permanent duty station (PDS), Meals
    and Incidental Expenses (M&IE), and lodging. These issues point to a systemic
    failure by Certifying Officers to perform basic validation of rates and calculations
    before approving vouchers for payment. This category of error represents a direct
    loss of taxpayer funds and is a primary reason the program is designated as an
    OMB High-Priority Program.


    A separate, but equally critical, issue is the high volume of "unknown" payments,
    which total over $120 million year-to-date. The root cause for this is a fundamental
    breakdown in pre-payment internal controls, specifically the failure of certifying
    officers to verify that required supporting documentation, such as receipts, is
    attached to travel vouchers before approval. According to the Corrective Action
    Plan, this procedural failure, where vouchers are marked "pay ready" without mandatory
    receipts, is the principal driver of unknown payments. According to PIIA guidelines,
    a payment that cannot be substantiated with documentation is classified as an
    improper payment, even if the payment was otherwise valid.


    These two primary issues are exacerbated by systemic and data-related problems.
    The DTS system itself lacks hard-coded controls or hard-stop edits to prevent
    the submission and approval of vouchers that are missing mandatory documentation
    or contain clear calculation errors; the system currently provides only soft warnings
    that can be bypassed. This technological gap enables persistent human non-compliance.
    Furthermore, a lack of seamless data integration between DTS and systems that
    house source documents like travel authorizations frequently results in incomplete
    records. This makes it impossible for post-payment auditors to verify the legality
    and accuracy of payments, thus categorizing them as "unknown."
