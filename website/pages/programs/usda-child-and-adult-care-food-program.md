  Program_Additional_Information: The Food and Nutrition Administration (FNA) conducted
    several studies over several years to identify a reliable method for measuring
    Child and Adult Care Food Program (CACFP) Family Day Care Home (FDCH) meal counting
    error.  To date, this effort has not generated a cost-effective solution to the
    agency’s improper payment reporting challenge. Following consultation with USDA's
    Office of Inspector General and the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), FNA
    is reporting an interim estimate of FDCH meal counting error for Fiscal Year (FY)
    2026 reporting, informed by the findings and limitations of past research. In
    the recent Family Day Care Homes Meal Claims Feasibility Study, the meal claiming
    error rates were 20.0% and 18.9% from two different approaches using the data
    collected by the study. However, the sampling method was not nationally representative
    and these results are not generalizable, as the study was meant to serve as a
    feasibility test. Additionally, these estimates include a very broad measure of
    overclaims - one that would be most effectively addressed with targeted technical
    assistance rather denial or federal reimbursement in many cases. After discussion
    with OMB, FNA is using 20.0% as our meal counting error rate in FDCHs for FY 2026
    reporting. This is the highest estimate from recent feasibility studies, so the
    agency believes it places an upper bound on improper payments due to meal counting
    error. As FNA continues to research approaches to measuring meal claiming error,
    the agency will consider this an interim estimate and will replace it once a more
    suitable method is established.

  detail_IP_Causes: 'USDA reimburses participating Child and Adult Care Food Program
    (CACFP) providers for the cost of serving program meals to eligible children.
    USDA does not reimburse providers directly. Instead, USDA issues payment to the
    states. The states then redistribute those funds to CACFP program sponsors and
    independent centers. Sponsors reimburse centers and day care homes under their
    supervision.


    A significant source of program error is committed by household applicants for
    program benefits and the sponsors who process those applications. Sponsoring organizations
    also make errors in determining whether day care homes and certain child care
    centers are located in area eligible sites; area eligibility determines the level
    of reimbursement that this subset of providers are entitled to for each program
    meal.


    Additional error occurs in the process of counting and aggregating meals for federal
    reimbursement. Meal aggregation is a responsibility of all program sponsors and
    independent centers.'
