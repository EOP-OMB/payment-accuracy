  Program_Additional_Information: "The following information provides context on the\
    \ statutory limits that prevent HHS from calculating and reporting an improper\
    \ or unknown payment error rate for the Temporary Assistance for Needy Families\
    \ (TANF) program, as well as HHS’ efforts to strengthen payment integrity. \n\n\
    TANF is a $16.5 billion block grant program that promotes family economic security\
    \ and stability. It funds services that support four broad purposes: \n\nAssisting\
    \ needy families so children can be cared for in their own homes; \n\nReducing\
    \ dependency by promoting job preparation, work, and marriage; \n\nPreventing\
    \ and reducing out-of-wedlock pregnancies; and \n\nEncouraging the formation and\
    \ maintenance of two-parent families. \n\nEstablished under section 401 of the\
    \ Social Security Act, TANF’s broad use-of-funds policy gives states flexibility\
    \ to design programs tailored to their populations’ needs. However, sections 411\
    \ and 417 of the Act limit the data HHS can collect, excluding information needed\
    \ to calculate an improper or unknown payment error rate. \n\nDespite these limitations,\
    \ HHS is committed to ensuring TANF funds are used as authorized by Congress and\
    \ to strengthening fiscal and program integrity. HHS has used a multi-faceted\
    \ approach to improving TANF\nprogram integrity and reducing the likelihood of\
    \ improper payments through actions such as risk assessments. Information from\
    \ these assessments helps to identify areas for risk mitigation and is used to\
    \ inform refinements to the multi-faceted approach to supporting states. HHS continues\
    \ to use data reporting and technical assistance to support states. ACF provides\
    \ technical assistance to grantees to support prevention of fraud, waste, and\
    \ abuse, sharing tools and resources to aid a range of efforts from restrictions\
    \ on and monitoring of Electronic Benefit Transfer (EBT) cards, sub-recipient\
    \ monitoring, data matching, and eligibility verification. In addition, HHS encourages\
    \ states to leverage existing data sources, such as the Public Assistance Reporting\
    \ Information System, the National Directory of New Hires, and the Income and\
    \ Eligibility Verification System, to help reduce improper payments."
