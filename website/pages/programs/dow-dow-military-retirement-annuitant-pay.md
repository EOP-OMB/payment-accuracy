  detail_IP_Causes: 'During the FY2026 Payment Integrity Information Act (PIIA) review,
    a statistically significant sample of 1,120 payments in the Military Retirement
    - Annuitant Pay program revealed an estimated improper plus unknown payment rate
    of 1.53% and an estimated $77.2M in improper and unknown payments. Improper and
    unknown payments were categorized into four primary root cause types: Monetary
    Loss to the Agency (MLA), Unknown Payments (UP), Underpayments (UN), and Technically
    Improper Payments.


    Monetary Losses to the Agency (MLA) represented the highest volume and financial
    impact, totaling an estimated $46.4M. The primary root causes for these losses
    were administrative and manual processing errors. By extrapolating the sampled
    error ratios against the projected total, specific scenarios driving these losses
    were payments being released in error (projected at approximately $20.1M), invalid
    forms being erroneously processed as valid (projected at $7.2M), and failures
    to recover debt prior to the release of annuity funds (projected at $5.5M).


    Unknown Payments (UP) represented the second highest category, totaling $25.8M
    in projected errors. The overarching root cause for unknown payments was the lack
    of sufficient documentation on file to properly support the computation of, or
    entitlement to, the issued payment. Specific scenarios driving these unknown payments
    included missing records where the date or manner of a member''s death could not
    be verified (projected at $9.7M), general insufficient documentation (projected
    at $10.9M), and missing documentation specifically concerning child annuitants
    (projected at $5.1M).


    Underpayments (UN) formed another key category, consisting of 16 errors that totaled
    $4.2M in projected errors. These improper payments were driven by calculation
    and data entry errors, specifically miscalculations of the Gross Annuity. The
    specific scenarios causing these underpayments were primarily driven by agents
    utilizing an incorrect base amount or level of coverage (projected at $3.56M).
    Finally, Technically Improper Payments accounted for $812,773 in projected errors,
    all of which shared a single root cause and scenario: incorrect or unverified
    payment addresses.
