  Corrective_Actions_Adequacy_Association_Implementation: The corrective actions are
    implemented by the Children’s Bureau (CB) through training, ongoing technical
    support, Title IV-E eligibility reviews, and targeted follow-up with jurisdictions,
    which determine a child’s eligibility and claim foster care funds. The work is
    prioritized by providing broad training and technical assistance to support consistent
    application of Title IV-E requirements and more focused assistance when review
    findings identify deficiencies. When a jurisdiction is determined not to be in
    substantial compliance in a Title IV-E Foster Care Eligibility Review, CB works
    with the jurisdiction to develop and implement a Program Improvement Plan addressing
    jurisdiction-specific eligibility and claiming issues. These program-wide actions
    provide multiple levels of prevention, detection, and targeted correction and
    are designed to adequately address the administrative errors that contribute to
    improper payments and reduce future claiming errors.                                                                                                                                                       The
    most recently reported error rate prior to the pause in Title IV-E reviews was
    3.36 percent for FY 2020. Because foster care improper payment estimates are developed
    through Title IV-E reviews conducted across jurisdictions over a rolling three-year
    cycle, CB is still establishing the current national improper payment rate as
    states are incorporated into the new review cycle. Therefore, the impact of the
    current corrective actions on the national improper payment rate cannot yet be
    fully determined. The impact of the corrective actions will be more fully assessed
    during the second round of Title IV-E reviews beginning in FY 2027. Once all states
    are represented in the sample and the current rate is established, CB will determine
    an appropriate target reduction rate and will be better positioned to assess the
    effectiveness of the corrective actions in reducing or maintaining the improper
    payment rate.

    and claiming requirements. Given the $110.5 million associated with the identified
    root causes and corrective actions, CB uses multiple program-wide corrective actions
    that collectively address prevention, detection, and correction of eligibility

    eligibility determinations and claims by states. Training provided before Title
    IV-E reviews also supports consistent application of review requirements and improves
    reviewers’ ability to identify eligibility and claiming errors. Ongoing technical
    assistance and Program Improvement Plans provide more targeted intervention when
    review findings identify jurisdiction-specific deficiencies, or a jurisdiction
    is not in substantial compliance. Payment-history reviews further strengthen oversight
    by identifying potential disallowances and improper payments before and during
    on-site reviews and providing states with information regarding correct claiming
    requirements. Because CB’s corrective actions are implemented program-wide, individual
    corrective actions are not assigned to a specific root-cause amount or rate; rather,
    the actions collectively provide different levels of prevention, oversight, and
    corrective action in response to identified payment risks and review findings.

  IP_Accountability_Description: Title IV-E Foster Care funds are claimed at the jurisdictional
    level, and state child welfare agencies are responsible for making accurate eligibility
    determinations and ensuring that claims for federal reimbursement comply with
    Title IV-E requirements. ACF holds jurisdictions accountable for those responsibilities
    primarily through Title IV-E Foster Care Eligibility Reviews, which assess whether
    eligibility determinations and associated claims comply with federal requirements.
    During these reviews, overpayments and underpayments identified in the sampled
    cases are documented, and improperly claimed federal funds are subject to disallowance
    and recovery by ACF. When a jurisdiction is determined not to be in substantial
    compliance, the jurisdiction must address identified eligibility and claiming
    deficiencies through the applicable corrective action process, including development
    and implementation of a Program Improvement Plan. Independent audits at the jurisdictional
    level provide an additional mechanism for identifying compliance and claiming
    issues.                                                                                                                                                                                                          Within
    ACF, the Children’s Bureau (CB), in coordination with the Office of Grants Management,
    provides federal oversight through eligibility reviews, payment-history analysis,
    technical assistance, corrective action, and follow-up on identified deficiencies.
    This oversight structure allows ACF to monitor state compliance, identify improper
    claims, recover disallowed federal funds, and require corrective action when deficiencies
    are identified. Because states are responsible for the accuracy of their Title
    IV-E eligibility determinations and claims, CB’s payment integrity role is focused
    on federal oversight and holding jurisdictions accountable for compliance with
    Title IV-E requirements; CB is currently establishing the national improper payment
    rate through the current review cycle and will determine an appropriate reduction
    target after the new baseline is established.
