  detail_IP_Causes: 'Summer EBT (SEBT) agencies certify children for program benefits
    by confirming that they were certified for free or reduced-price school meal benefits
    during the school year that immediately preceded the summer. National School Lunch
    (NSLP) participating school districts certify children for school meal benefits
    by application or a process of direct certification that confirms participation
    in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program or other means-tested programs.


    For children not certified by their schools for NSLP benefits, SEBT agencies offer
    households a dedicated SEBT application. SEBT agencies also search the participation
    records of means-tested programs for any remaining school-age children not identified
    through NSLP direct certification.


    The primary driver of improper payments in the SEBT certification process are
    errors committed by household applicants and processing errors by school districts
    and SEBT agencies. Errors by school districts in the direct certification process,
    and errors by SEBT agencies in identifying additional participants in eligible
    means-tested programs contribute much less to the overall SEBT certification error
    rate. In each case, the root cause of error is the failure to access, manage,
    or submit data or other eligibility information.


    Additional errors, not connected to the certification process, contribute a much
    smaller amount to SEBT improper payments. These errors include duplicate issuances,
    payments in the wrong amount, or payments to ineligible children. This broad category
    of issuance errors is also driven by a failure to access or properly use administrative
    data.
