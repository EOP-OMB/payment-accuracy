detail_DNP_Discussion: "The Department of State (the Department) uses the Department\
  \ of the Treasury's Do Not Pay (DNP) Working System to prevent and detect improper\
  \ payments before and after disbursement. DNP has reduced improper payments at the\
  \ Department. \n\nIn FY 2026, Treasury’s Payment Verification replaced the legacy\
  \ Payment Integration that was previously in the Do Not Pay Web Portal. As part\
  \ of the Payment Verification, agencies are no longer required to adjudicate death\
  \ matches in the DNP portal. Instead, the Payments module in the Do Not Pay Web\
  \ Portal was redesigned with the Treasury’s Bureau of the Fiscal Service (BFS) Payment\
  \ Verification (PV) screening initiative, allowing users to easily interpret and\
  \ view PV screening results. The Department is utilizing this new DNP Web Portal\
  \ for online searches and the legacy system for other screenings. Death Screening\
  \ went online in January. Treasury granted the Department an extension until December\
  \ 31, 2026, to implement Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) payment verification.\
  \ \n\nIn FY 2026, the Department soft launched Death Screening, verifying 1,061,997\
  \ payments totaling $11.66 billion, with 21 returned payments totaling $109,518.\
  \ The Department performed 869 DNP Web Portal online searches, as needed, and on\
  \ a daily and monthly basis screened 50,511,204 records with 43,558 match results\
  \ through the Legacy system. \n\nAt least twice each month, the Department screens\
  \ its annuitant database against the Death Master File (DMF) to identify deceased\
  \ annuitants. The Department researches every match and, once it confirms a match,\
  \ stops the disbursement before the monthly annuity payment is processed. In FY\
  \ 2026, the Department screened 216,782 annuitant payments, totaling $1.3 billion,\
  \ and stopped 534 payments, totaling $2.8 million. The Department also checks every\
  \ manual annuity payment processed through Treasury's Secure Payment System against\
  \ the DMF before it releases payment. \n\nFor overseas payments issued by non-Treasury\
  \ disbursing offices, the Department screens payee information against the Treasury\
  \ Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list.\
  \ In FY 2026, the Department screened 2,215,171 payments, totaling $7.9 billion.\
  \ None of these payments required a stop. \n\nThe Department processes grant payments\
  \ through the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Payment Management System\
  \ (PMS). HHS uses the DNP portal to identify recipients who owe delinquent federal\
  \ non-tax debt, are listed as deceased on the DMF, or are excluded from doing business\
  \ with the federal government. In FY 2026, HHS found no ineligible Department grant\
  \ recipients. In FY 2026, the Treasury Offset Program (TOP) stopped one recipient\
  \ payment. TOP is administered by Treasury's Bureau of Fiscal Service. It collects\

detail_Overpayment_Conditions_And_Methods: "The Department of State's Bureau of the\
  \ Comptroller and Global Financial Services (CGFS) provides financial management\
  \ services to the Department and other agencies. CGFS monitors improper payments\
  \ through a two-tiered review process. First, payment-issuing offices review every\
  \ payment before disbursement. Second, CGFS’ Office of Governance and Oversight\
  \ (CGFS/GOV) conducts internal post-payment audits, using random and targeted samples\
  \ of domestic, overseas, and employee payments. \n\nDomestic vendor payments make\
  \ up the largest category of Department payments subject to Payment Integrity Information\
  \ Act (PIIA) recovery audit requirements. Each month, CGFS/GOV runs an automated\
  \ comparison of vendor invoice data against prior-year payments and flags potential\
  \ duplicates for recapture audit analysis. This automated process has proven a cost-effective\
  \ way to review domestic payments. Each quarter, CGFS/GOV also manually reviews\
  \ samples of domestic and overseas payments. It reviews supporting documentation\
  \ to confirm that the Department paid the correct vendor the correct amount. \n\n\
  CGFS/GOV also audits employee payments each quarter, including permanent change\
  \ of station (PCS) travel, temporary duty (TDY) travel, tuition and education allowances,\
  \ representation expenses, local transportation, and other reimbursements. These\
  \ audits review supporting documentation to confirm that payments are accurate and\
  \ comply with regulations. \n\nHistorically, the most common causes of potential\
  \ improper payments were caused by insufficient supporting documentation or a misinterpretation\
  \ of applicable regulations. Generally, only minimal amounts have been referred\
  \ for recapture because of recovery audits. In FY 2026, the Department of State\
  \ identified $0 in improper payments through recovery audits. \n\nCGFS continues\
  \ to review these processes and, where possible, uses automation and super intelligence\
  \ (SI) tools to make them more efficient and effective."
