detail_Executive_Summary: "The Payment Integrity Information Act of 2019 (PIIA) was\
  \ established to improve efforts to identify and reduce Government-wide improper\
  \ payments. PIIA requires agencies to estimate improper payments and report on actions\
  \ to reduce improper payments. In accordance with Office of Management and Budget\
  \ (OMB) Circular A-123, Appendix C, Requirements for Payment Integrity Improvement\
  \ (M-21-19) (hereafter referred to as “Appendix C”), agencies are required to identify,\
  \ assess, prioritize, and respond to payment integrity risks to prevent improper\
  \ payments in the most appropriate manner. This report will discuss the SBA’s efforts\
  \ and results related to improper payment assessment and reviews. More detailed\
  \ information on improper payments and information previously reported in the AFR\
  \ that is not included in the FY 2026 AFR can be found on PaymentAccuracy.gov. \n\
  \nThe SBA’s improper payment reviews are a multi-layered process that starts with\
  \ a risk assessment. If a risk assessment indicates that a program is susceptible\
  \ to improper payments, then testing is performed using a statistically valid sampling\
  \ technique. Based upon the testing results, a corrective action plan is developed\
  \ to prevent and remediate the types of errors uncovered. If testing finds a significant\
  \ amount of recoverable dollars, the SBA considers the appropriateness of performing\
  \ a recovery audit. \n\nThe detection and remediation of improper payments is a\
  \ priority for the Agency. SBA staff monitor and review for improper payments and\
  \ implement improvements that will reduce improper payment rates. The SBA also continually\
  \ seeks opportunities to enhance and implement internal controls to reduce the risk\
  \ of improper payments.  \n\nFor FY 2025 the Agency was found to be non-compliant\
  \ with PIIA due to improper and unknown payment rates exceeding the 10% threshold\
  \ in certain programs, inaccurate reporting, incomplete risk assessments, inadequately\
  \ supported payment estimates and corrective-action plans, missed reduction targets,\
  \ and failure to submit required information. \n\nTo begin with remedying the issues\
  \ identified by the auditors, SBA has instituted Corrective Action Plans (CAP's).\
  \ These plans are designed to address the findings above and bring the Agency into\
  \ compliance with PIIA. SBA anticipates achieving compliance for the audit of FY2027\
  \ activities.  \n\nThe SBA conducts risk assessments on a three-year rotation cycle\
  \ for programs not deemed susceptible to significant improper payments, or earlier\
  \ if a program was subjected to significant change in legislation or funding level.\
  \ Monitoring of off-cycle activity is also performed and responded to when significant\
  \ changes occur. The qualitative risk assessment applies an individual risk weight\
  \ to each risk factor to account for the variability in potential risk that each\
  \ risk factor could impose on a program. In FY 2026, the agency completed one risk\
  \ assessment over travel expenditures. The program was found to be not susceptible\
  \ to improper payments.  \n\nFor FY 2026, the SBA had 6 programs reporting Phase\
  \ II improper payment estimates: 504 Certified Development Companies (CDC) Loan\
  \ Approvals; 7(a) Loan Guarantee Approvals; 7(a) Loan Guarantee Purchases; Disaster\
  \ Assistance Loans; Paycheck Protection Plan (PPP) Loan Forgiveness; and PPP Loan\
  \ Purchases.  \n\nThe statistical approach remained consistent between FY 2025 and\
  \ FY 2026 for all the programs, with the exception for 504 Certified Development\
  \ Companies (CDC) Loan Approvals that began daily sample selection (instead of monthly)\
  \ for the remaining 3 months of the PIIA period. \n\n1.\t504 CDC Loan Approvals\
  \  \nFor FY 2026 504 Approvals, the Improper plus unknown rate was calculated at\
  \ 2.69%, for a total estimated amount of $203.77 million. Compared to the prior\
  \ reporting period, this was a reduction in both percentage and dollars, down from\
  \ 4.58% and $338.42 million in FY 2025.   \nThe primary causes of improper payments\
  \ included: CDCs’ failure to perform due diligence on borrowers and collateral and\
  \ CDCs’ failure to adequately address job creation requirements.  \nThe reduction\
  \ can be accredited to the Agency’s continuous quarterly lender training and quality\
  \ reviews by the Sacramento Loan Processing Center (SLPC), as most loans selected\
  \ for the PIIA reviews were submitted under lenders’ non-delegated authority.  \
  \ \nTo reduce future improper payments, a Corrective Action Plan has been formalized.\
  \  Specific corrective actions are determined based upon the primary reason for\
  \ the improper payment with the purpose of both remedying the improper payment and\
  \ preventing recurrence. \nPlans for improvement include the following: collaborating\
  \ with the Office of Credit Risk Management (OCRM) to recommend that specific lender\
  \ deficiencies be monitored and incorporated into Risk-Based Reviews; collaborating\
  \ with the Office of Financial Assistance (OFA) to inform the office of deficiencies\
  \ identified for potential incorporation into policy, regulatory, or standard operating\
  \ procedure rewrite or update; and conducting external training for lenders to ensure\
  \ that appropriate documentation is obtained and analyzed prior to loan approval\
  \ that policy requirements are met.  \nSBA has made multiple updates to PIIA review\
  \ notification and procedures related to 504 loan approvals. Per SOP 50 10 8 effective\
  \ 6/1/25, CDCs are responsible for ensuring there is no open PIIA review before\
  \ a 504 loan moves forward to closing. 504 loan approval samples are pulled multiple\
  \ times per week shortly after loan commitment and CDCs along with Third Party Lenders\
  \ (TPL) are notified promptly of selection.  All results are shared with both CDCs\
  \ and TPLs. There are also internal checks in place to ensure no 504 loan with an\
  \ open PIIA review moves toward closing. \n\n2.\t 7(a) Loan Guaranty Approvals \n\

  \ an estimated amount of $1985.9 million. Compared to the prior reporting period,\
  \ this is an increase from 6.13% ($1632.51 million) in FY 2025.  \nThe primary causes\
  \ of improper payments included lenders’ failure to verify all components of loan\
  \ eligibility (program integrity or project structure issues), failure to provide\
  \ required loan documentation, and data entry errors.  \nThis increase is attributed\
  \ to unintentional lender underwriting errors and insufficient documentation under\
  \ delegated processing. To address this, the program implemented updated SOP requirements\
  \ effective June 2025, reinstituted the SBA Franchise Directory with streamlined\

  \ OCRM for Risk-Based Reviews, collaborate with the Loan Guarantee Processing Center\

  \ external training to lenders on eligibility, structure, and documentation in FY\
  \ 2027 to achieve its target reduction rate. \n\n3.\t7(a) Loan Guaranty Purchases\
