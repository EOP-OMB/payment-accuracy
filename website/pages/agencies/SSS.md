  \ has implemented key payment verification controls to reduce the risk of improper\
  \ payments. As part of this process, IBC’s vendor management system continuously\

  \ identify vendors whose TINs do not meet Treasury’s requirements, helping prevent\
  \ payments to potentially fraudulent, deceased, or otherwise ineligible entities.\
  \ These upfront verification activities serve as critical internal controls that\
  \ reduce identity‑based payment risk and reinforce overall payment integrity.\n\n\

  \ enhanced payment verification process, Treasury has not flagged any payments for\
  \ our agency as \"Potential Returns\" against Death Screening rule and Bank Verification\

detail_Executive_Summary: "The Payment Integrity Information Act of 2019 (PIIA), effective\
  \ March 2, 2020, requires that agencies: (1) review programs' and activities' susceptibility\
  \ to significant improper payments; (2) estimate the annual improper payments for\
  \ high-risk programs and develop plans to reduce improper payments; and (3) report\
  \ the estimated amount of improper payments and the progress to reduce them. PIIA\
  \ defines “improper payment” as any payment that should not have been made or that\
  \ was made in an incorrect amount, including an overpayment, underpayment, or duplicate\
  \ payments, under statutory, contractual, administrative, or other legally applicable\
  \ requirements. The act defines “significant improper payments” as the gross annual\
  \ improper payments exceeding either: a) both $10 million of all reported program\
  \ or activity payments made during the fiscal year and 1.5 percent of program outlays;\
  \ or b) $100 million (regardless of the percentage of total program outlays).\n\n\
  \nTo strengthen payment integrity, SSS utilizes Department of Interior - Interior\
  \ Business Center (IBC) Oracle Federal Financials (OFF) shared services solution\
  \ to support financial management processes. SSS also mandates written justification\
  \ for all vendor and interagency payments, documenting purpose, authority, public\
  \ benefit and cost efficiently of the payment. Partnering with the Treasury, SSS\
  \ conducts regular checks against the Do Not Pay (DNP) portal and the System for\
  \ Award Management (SAM) to verify vendor eligibility and detect improper vendor\
  \ payments. Additionally, SSS processes vendor payments using the Treasury's Invoice\
  \ Processing Platform (IPP) to securely manage electronic invoicing, reduce manual\
  \ errors, and increase transparency. Lastly, SSS conducts annual external financial\
  \ audits, which review budget execution, control weakness, and non-compliance with\
  \ regulations. This external review enhances transparency, accountability, and confidence\
  \ in SSS's payment processes. \n\n\nAn independent external auditor conducted the\
  \ FY 2024 evaluation under the Payment Integrity Information Act (PIIA) and determined\
  \ that SSS complied with PIIA reporting requirements, including the accurate reporting\
  \ of information on paymentaccuracy.gov. Although SSS had not completed the required\
  \ improper payment risk assessment for its program with annual outlays exceeding\
  \ $10 million within the three-year period, the assessment was finalized during\
  \ the 2024 audit. Consequently, no corrective action was required. The assessment\
  \ concluded that the program was not susceptible to significant improper or unknown\
  \ payments exceeding the statutory threshold and remains classified as Phase I with\
  \ a low level of risk. SSS plans to continue its three-year PIIA review cycle, with\
  \ the next assessment scheduled for FY 2027, unless material changes necessitate\
  \ an earlier review. The results of the risk assessment are available on paymentaccuracy.gov.\n\
  \n"
detail_OIG_Recommendations: As an independent agent, SSS does not have an Office of
  Inspector General (OIG) to evaluate improper payments. However, an external auditor
  conducted the PIIA audit for FY 2024.  The audit concluded that SSS was compliant
  with the applicable PIIA requirements and submitted required information to paymentaccuracy.gov;
  however, SSS did not complete an improper payment risk assessment in a timely manner
  for the program with annual outlays greater than $10 million, as required at least
  once every three years. SSS finalized the risk assessment during the 2024 audit.
  Since the risk assessment completed during the audit, no further corrective actions
  were necessary. For the next risk assessment cycle, SSS will ensure that the assessment
  is completed on time to maintain compliance.

detail_PIIA_Incentives: The performance agreements for the responsible official do
  not contain explicit incentives or consequences tied directly to achieving Payment
  Integrity Information Act (PIIA) compliance. Instead, compliance responsibilities
  are embedded within established managerial performance expectations requiring officials
  to fulfill all statutory and regulatory requirements, including the timely remediation
  of deficiencies identified in payment‑integrity processes. In the absence of formal
  incentives or consequences, the official is expected to take timely and effective
  action and maintain oversight of corrective measures as part of routine management
  duties. Accordingly, the agency’s progress toward compliance is assessed through
  managerial accountability, internal oversight, collaboration with program offices,
  and a sustained commitment to meeting federal requirements rather than through performance‑based
  incentives or consequences.

detail_Recovery_Not_Cost_Effective_Justification: Considering the annual budget, routine
  financial and PIIA audits, and various system controls, SSS has determined that
  is not cost effective to conduct separate recovery audits in certain programs.  Although
  the PIIA audit is not performed annually, an independent external auditor reviews
  the disbursement process as part of the financial audit to verify the accuracy of
  payments. In addition, SSS utilizes IBC's Oracle Federal Financials (OFF) shared
  accounting and payroll services, which support payment processing for both vendors
  and employees. The OFF system includes multiple internal controls to prevent improper
  payments, such as approval workflows, user access controls, audit trails, three-way
  matching for vendors, exception reporting, vendor/employee master file controls,
  and reconciliation procedures.
