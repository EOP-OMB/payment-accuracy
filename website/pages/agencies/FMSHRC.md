  During FY 2026, the Commission continued strengthening its financial management

  of the Treasury and the General Services Administration (GSA).

  prior-year locality-pay overpayments through administrative recovery activities
  rather than formal recovery audits. Additionally, no improper payments were identified

  activities demonstrate the Commission''s ongoing efforts to identify, address, and
  prevent improper payments through financial oversight and established administrative
  controls.


  In response to GAO’s recommendation to establish a comprehensive agencywide Enterprise
  Risk Management (ERM) process in GAO-25-107171,   FMSHRC plans to implement an agency-wide

  of FY 2027.  This initiative will include Phase 1 improper payment risk assessments,
  internal control evaluations, identification of potential payment vulnerabilities,
  corrective action planning, employee and supervisory training, and ongoing compliance
  monitoring.


  Through continued coordination with Treasury, GSA, and the Office of Management
  and Budget (OMB), FMSHRC will work to enhance financial oversight, improve payment
  accuracy, and establish sustainable internal controls that promote effective stewardship
  of federal funds.'

detail_Executive_Summary: 'During Fiscal Year (FY) 2026, the Federal Mine Safety and
  Health Review Commission (FMSHRC) continued efforts to strengthen payment integrity,
  improve financial accountability, and maintain effective internal controls over
  agency expenditures in accordance with the Payment Integrity Information Act of
  2019 (PIIA), OMB Circular A-123, Appendix C, and applicable federal financial management
  requirements.


  FMSHRC is a small, independent federal agency with no programs designated for Phase
  2 improper payment reporting or high-priority improper payment reporting during
  FY 2026. The Commission has not previously completed a formal Phase 1 improper payment
  risk assessment and is taking steps to establish a more comprehensive, risk-based
  payment integrity framework.


  During FY 2026, FMSHRC successfully recovered $5,469.23 in prior-year locality-pay
  improper payments associated with a personnel action involving a prohibited personnel
  practice. The Commission evaluated related hiring and pay-setting controls and implemented
  measures intended to reduce the likelihood of similar improper payments, including
  employee training and improvements associated with the transition to a new human
  resources shared-service provider.


  As part of its ongoing administrative modernization efforts, FMSHRC transitioned
  certain human resources and payroll functions to the General Services Administration
  (GSA), while continuing to obtain financial management and budgetary services from
  the Department of the Treasury. These shared-service arrangements are intended to
  strengthen operational oversight, standardize administrative processes, and support
  compliance with applicable federal financial management requirements.


  In response to GAO’s recommendation to establish a comprehensive agencywide Enterprise
  Risk Management (ERM) process in GAO-25-107171,   FMSHRC plans to implement an agency-wide
  ERM initiative encompassing all programs and operations beginning in the third quarter
  of FY 2027.  This initiative will incorporate Phase 1 improper payment risk assessments,
  evaluations of internal controls, identification of potential payment vulnerabilities,
  corrective action planning, employee and supervisory training, and ongoing compliance
  monitoring


  The Commission will also continue coordinating with its financial management shared-service
  provider to verify its use of Treasury''s Do Not Pay services, assess applicable
  payment-screening procedures, and identify opportunities to strengthen improper
  payment prevention and detection.


  FMSHRC is working to complete applicable OMB payment integrity reporting requirements
  and establish the necessary disclosures in its Performance and Accountability Report
  (PAR). Once the Commission''s agency link on PaymentAccuracy.gov is activated and
  applicable reports are completed and made available, FMSHRC will provide the appropriate
  references and instructions for accessing its payment integrity information.


  Through these ongoing and planned activities, FMSHRC remains committed to strengthening
  financial stewardship, improving internal controls, preventing and recovering improper
  payments, and promoting transparency and accountability in the use of federal resources.'
