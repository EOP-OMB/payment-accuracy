detail_DNP_Discussion: The EEOC is cross-serviced by the Department of Interior (DOI),
  Interior Business Center (IBC) for accounting system support and accounts payable
  processing. As a result, the implementation of the Do Not Pay (DNP) Initiative is
  a joint responsibility between the EEOC and IBC. Prior to making a new contract
  award, the EEOC checks the System for Award Management (SAM) and the Excluded Parties
  List System (EPLS) for a match. If there is no match, the EEOC submits a new vendor
  request to IBC. The IBC Vendor Maintenance Team verifies the EEOC’s entire new employee
  and non-federal vendor requests against the Department of Treasury’s DNP database
  using the DNP portal on-line search capability. If the IBC Vendor Maintenance Team
  finds a positive match, the EEOC is notified. The EEOC reviews the match, determines
  if the payment is proper, and reports the result.  IBC’s Oracle Federal Financial
  (OFF) solution utilizes Treasury’s “Online Search” and “Continuous Monitoring”.
  These services help identify suppliers that may need to be inactivated in OFF and
  support the prevention of improper payments based on this data.  IBC’s Vendor Management
  process has been enhanced to proactively end-date the suppliers matched through
  the Do Not Pay Screening rules. As a result of this enhancement, we have not encountered
  any records with “Potential Returns” in production for our agency. All Non-Federal
  and Employee suppliers are interfaced to Treasury’s DNP portal once every week.
  In FY26, 9,558 payments or $52,942,068.26, were reviewed and no improper payments
  were identified. The IBC team also continues to monitor for any “Potential Returns”
  identified through Bank Account Validations and has implemented processes to notify
  the appropriate agency points of contact so the underlying issues can be resolved
  and prevented from recurring in production.

detail_Executive_Summary: In fiscal year 2026, the EEOC conducted Improper Payment
  Testing over its transaction universe in accordance with OMB Circular A-123, Appendix
  C, OMB Memorandum M-21-19, Section VIII, and standard operating procedures. Accordingly,
  in fiscal year 2026, the EEOC conducted scheduled reviews and audits of payroll,
  vendor payments, and travel payments. Moreover, the agency administered a payment
  integrity survey across programs and determined that the agency has an overall low
  risk rating for fraud and improper payments. In FY 2026, the Office of Inspector
  General deemed the EEOC compliant with the Payment Integrity Information Act (PIIA)
  in accordance with Office of Management and Budget Memorandum M-21-19. Additionally,
  a 2024 Pay and Benefits Risk Assessment and a 2025 Risk Assessment of the Travel
  and Vendor Payments Program, which includes Other Contractual Services, Rental Payments,
  and State and Local Programs determined that the assessed programs are not susceptible
  to significant improper payments or unknown payments. Based on the results of the
  FY 26 Payment Integrity Survey, quarterly transaction testing applied to a sample
  of payments, consideration of risk factors, and reliance on the internal controls
  in place over the payment process, the EEOC determined that none of its programs
  and activities are susceptible to significant improper or unknown payments at or
  above the threshold levels set by PIIA. The EEOC will conduct another review of
  its programs in fiscal year 2027 to determine whether the programs have experienced
  any unexpected changes. If so, the EEOC will assess program risk susceptibility
  and make a statistically valid estimate of improper payments for any programs determined
  to be susceptible to significant erroneous payments.
