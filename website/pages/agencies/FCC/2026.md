detail_DNP_Discussion: The FCC has established several critical financial controls
  in place, including the US Treasury Do Not Pay (DNP) system. The agency utilizes
  DNP to verify the eligibility of payment recipients before disbursing funds as well
  as when onboarding new programs and vendors, thereby reducing the risk of improper
  payments. This has led to a reduced concerns in award processing. DNP is also used
  prior to making an award. For example, if a vendor does not have an active SAM.gov
  registration shown in the DNP system then the Commission will not issue the award.
  Although the Commission does not actively track the amount of savings as a result
  of using the DNP system, this is something that we will look to track in the future.

detail_Executive_Summary: "In accordance with the Payment Integrity Information Act\
  \ (PIIA) of 2019, the Commission has made significant efforts to implement policies\
  \ and procedures to strengthen internal controls that prevent improper payments.\
  \ In addition, the Commission utilizes a payment recapture program that includes\
  \ both audits and transaction testing to search for and recapture overpayments.\
  \ Although the Commission has made strides to improve policies, procedures and controls\
  \ over improper payments, there is still more work to be done. For FY 2026, the\
  \ Commission identified three phase 2 programs that meet the reporting criteria:\
  \ Universal Service Fund (USF)-Lifeline, USF-High Cost Legacy, and Secured & Trusted\
  \ Communications Network Reimbursement Program (STCNRP). \n\nUSF-High Cost Legacy:\
  \ Although the program is still non-compliant with the PIIA of 2019, USF-High Cost\
  \ Legacy remains a complex program, the improper payment rate has decreased from\
  \ 3.26% to 1.82%. Strides have continued to be made to improve our efforts to reduce\
  \ the improper payment rate. The Commission will continue to work on decreasing\
  \ the amount of carriers using the incorrect depreciation method. This exception\
  \ type remains the core issue that the Commission is actively working to address.\
  \ In addition, special compliance reviews will continue to be made to identify methods\
  \ to reduce the root causes of the improper payment rate. In the interim, corrective\
  \ actions will continue to be taken such as using Risk Management Council Meetings\
  \ to discuss strategic initiatives and risks, as well as conduct its annual Circle\
  \ of Life webinars. The Commission is hopeful that the USF-High Cost Legacy Program\
  \ will be compliant with the next couple of years.\n\nUSF-Lifeline: Unfortunately,\
  \ the USF-Lifeline program remains non-compliant with the PIIA of 2019, as the improper\
  \ rate estimate increased from 9.12% to 14.45%. The major causes of the improper\
  \ payments are inadequate documentation related to usage. The major causes make\
  \ up 73% of the improper payment rate. Examples of why the documentation was inadequate\
  \ may include, but is not limited to, no usage during the 30 days prior to the snapshot\
  \ date. The documentation provided does not demonstrate qualified usage during the\
  \ 30 days prior to the snapshot date. The Commission and our Administrator, the\
  \ Universal Service Administrative Company (USAC), the Lifeline program management\
  \ continues to develop multiple outreach activities, including updates to the website\
  \ training materials, conducting webinar(s) focused on common audit findings, prior\
  \ program quality assurance (PQA) reviews and communicating best practices based\
  \ on the observations. The Commission remains optimistic that the Lifeline Program\
  \ will be compliant with the next couple of years.\n\nSTCNRP: FY 2026 was the first\
  \ year that the Commission identified STCNRP as a phase 2 program; the program was\
  \ determined to be compliant with the PIIA of 2019. Through testing, the improper\
  \ payment rate estimate was determined to be 0.13%. This rate speaks to the sound\
  \ procedures, practices, and systems in place that have led to the program being\
  \ under the 1.5% threshold. Out of the sample identified, only one improper payment\
  \ was identified. This payment was a duplicative payment that has since been corrected.\n\
  \ \nThe Commission and its components continue to make great strides when it comes\
  \ to oversight, internal control updates and implementation of policies and procedures.\
  \ Our ultimate goal is to bring all of our programs back to phase 1 reporting requirements\
  \ although there is more work to be done in order to reach the goal. With the Commission\
  \ leadership, we continue to make much needed improvements in all of our programs\
  \ each year. The Commission takes seriously the recommendations provided by our\
  \ OIG and its auditor so that our programs are in compliance with the guidance outlined\
  \ in OMB M-21-19.\n\nIn addition, the FCC does conduct Phase 1 risk assessment on\
  \ a three-year cycle, unless the program has made significant changes in policy\
  \ or funding. For FY 2026, the Commission did conduct four Phase 1 risk assessments\
  \ for Telecommunications Relay Service, Federal Communications Commission Operating\
  \ Expenses, USF-Cybersecurity Pilot Program, and USAC-Administrative Costs. All\
  \ programs were deemed not susceptible to significant risk. Therefore, these programs\
  \ will remain Phase 1 programs."
