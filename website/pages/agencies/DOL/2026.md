detail_Executive_Summary: "DOL places the highest priority on payment integrity and\
  \ the stewardship of taxpayer dollars. Waste, fraud, and abuse are not acceptable.\

  \ conduct annual Program Inventory Questionnaires to survey their IP risk posture.\
  \ No less than every three years each identified program is required to conduct\
  \ a detailed Risk Assessment which identifies if any may be \"susceptible to significant\
  \ improper payments\" on both quantitative and qualitative factors on a fiscal year\
  \ basis. For FY26, PIIA-identified programs with outlays >$1OM conducted risk assessments\
  \ are reported on PaymentAccuracy.gov. Programs found to have either IP >1.5% of\
  \ total annual outlays and >$10M, or >$100M annually are \"susceptible\" and required\
  \ to provide annual reporting. In FY26 DOL continued to find two programs to be\
  \ \"susceptible\".\n•\tFederal-State Unemployment Insurance (UI) program, and\n\
  •\tOffice of Workers' Compensation (OWCP) - Federal Employees' Compensation Act\
  \ (FECA) program.\nDOL did not determine any additional programs to be \"susceptible\
  \ to significant IP\" in FY26. Both of these programs provide IP estimates based\
  \ on a program year (PY) basis, July 1 - June 30. For FY26, the FECA program's IP\

  \ IP rate was estimated at 12.40%, down from 13.14% in FY25. While the goal is always\

  \ states, under state law, using primarily state UI funds, but under oversight by\
  \ DOL to ensure compliance with Federal law. DOL will continue efforts to implement\

  \ IP reduction strategies, targeting the leading root causes of IP. This includes\

detail_Recovery_Justifications_Audits: State UI programs may determine an overpayment
  to be uncollectable. Generally, this means that all reasonable collection efforts
  have been exhausted and the overpayment has been officially written off and/or authorized
  for removal from the active accounts receivable file and transferred to suspense
  (no further action to be taken). In cases involving UI identity fraud, when the
  perpetrator of fraud is unknown, a state may also identify the overpayment as "uncollectible",
  as recovery efforts could not be initiated until the perpetrator of fraud is identified.
  If the perpetrator of fraud is not identified and the state's law authorizes overpayments
  to be written off, the state is permitted to write-off the overpayment in accordance
  with their state law. Usually, write-offs are applied after the statute of limitations
  expires, bankruptcy has been approved by a court, or the party responsible for the
  overpayment has died. Even though an overpayment amount may be deemed not collectable,
  states are still expected to collaborate with local, state, and Federal law enforcement
  to pursue criminal prosecution where appropriate.
