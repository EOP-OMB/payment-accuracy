  as appropriate. Payment integration consists of matching payments against available
  databases at the time of disbursement. The Department of Treasury leveraged the
  DNP Portal to provide Payment Verification (PV) Results in FY 2026 that provides
  summary level payments screened via Payment verification. Prior to June 30, 2026,
  VA adjudicated matches within the Treasury Working System monthly. With the implementation
  of PV, the adjudication process is no longer required as payments are either stopped
  or processed according to VA established business rules. This includes total payments
  screened, matched, potential returned payments, returned payments, and warnings
  for payment matches. According to the dashboard, VA screened 125.38 million payments
  totaling $331.8 billion in the DNP Portal and the Department of Treasury returned
  about 2,000 payments or $6.03 million in FY 2026. Additionally, VA maintains a fraud
  outlook when reviewing returned payments for deceased beneficiaries and makes referrals
  to the Office of Inspector General when suspicious behavior is suspected.


  Pre-eligibility entails VA matching its Vendor File to the Death Master File monthly
  to determine whether a vendor is deceased. If a vendor is deceased, the vendor file
  is blocked for any future payment. The Vendor File is a complete list of active
  VA Vendors (i.e., employees, commercial vendors, medical providers, Veterans, caregivers,
  federal vendors, etc.) who have been paid by VA within a certain timeframe by vendor
  type (approximately 3 years). VA is currently working with Treasury to perform an
  Account Verification System and Electronic Verification of Vital Events check on
  all vendor records and any subsequent new ones.

  The Veterans Benefits Administration Life Insurance for Veterans – Direct Payments
  for Insurance program uses Do Not Pay at pre-eligibility to perform single-searches
  within the Treasury Working system daily. The program reviews the data to confirm
  individuals are not deceased. There is no report available within the Treasury Working

  prevented. Program personnel have also worked with the Department of Treasury to

  \ to risk assessment requirements with 31 programs completing an improper and unknown\
  \ payment risk assessment. VA programs complete risk assessments based on the previous\
  \ fiscal year’s spending and activity to ensure all spending is captured and thresholds\
  \ are correctly identified. In addition, VA programs complete risk assessments no\
  \ less than every three years as required by PIIA but often more frequently than\

  \ or spending to determine if programs should complete a full risk assessment earlier\

  \ below. \n\nVeterans Health Administration \nThe Veterans Health Administration\
  \ utilizes a recovery audit contract, which will audit claims for three programs:\
  \ Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs, Purchased\
  \ Long Term Services and Supports, and VA Community Care. Payments were tested to\
  \ ensure the reimbursement was based on the appropriate methodology and that the\
  \ medical records support the diagnostic related group billed for the services.\
  \ \n\nDuring FY 2026, the recovery audit identified overpayments where the medical\
  \ record did not substantiate the services billed or the coding was not accurate\
  \ based on a review of documents. Solutions to resolve these conditions are being\
  \ developed but will not be implemented until FY 2027. The identified overpayments\
  \ resulted in re-adjudication of the original payments in the claims processing\
  \ system, leading to bills of collections being issued to the community care providers.\
  \ Additional methods used to recover overpayments include internal off-sets and\
  \ Treasury’s Invoice Payment Processing Payment off-set.\n\nOffice of Revolving\
  \ Funds \nThe Office of Revolving Funds collaborates with the VA Office of Inspector\
  \ General to perform recovery audits on VA's Supply Fund program. Overpayments identified\
  \ are mostly due to non-compliance with contract billing requirements on federal\
  \ supply schedule contracts. These conditions are being resolved by education provided\
  \ by the VA Office of Inspector General to non-conforming vendors. The VA Office\
  \ of Inspector General identifies an overpayment, and a bill of collection is then\
  \ prepared by the Office of Revolving Funds with appropriate terms of collection.\
  \ If the vendor chooses to dispute the bill of collection VA will work towards appropriate\
  \ resolution."

detail_Recovery_Not_Cost_Effective_Justification: VA programs that have determined
  that it is not cost-effective to conduct recovery audits have documented their justifications
  through a cost benefit analysis. VA has determined it would not be cost-effective
  to perform recovery audits for the majority of its programs. Whenever possible,
  VA utilizes quantitative factors, such as recovery amounts and actual costs incurred
  in recovery audits to determine if the benefits of a payment recovery audit program
  would exceed the costs associated with identification and recovery of overpayments.
  Lacking full quantitative data, VA will also utilize qualitative factors to determine
  if the benefits of a payment recovery audit program would exceed the costs associated
  with identification and recovery of overpayments. Qualitative factors evaluate the
  likelihood that identified overpayments would be recovered and the likelihood that
  the expected recoveries would be greater than the costs incurred to identify and
  recover overpayments. Each of the 90 programs determined that conducting a recovery
  audit would not be cost effective due to the costs or expected costs of the related
  recovery audit outweighing the collectible overpayments.
